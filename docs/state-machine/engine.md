# Transition engine
{: .no_toc }

The [lifecycle table]({{ '/state-machine/table/' | relative_url }}) defines every state and transition of a contribution. `engine.py` applies it: `engine.apply_transition(record, verb, ...)` is the write path of the lifecycle fields `phase`, `activity`, `paused`, `revision`, and `generation`. This page describes how a transition is taken, which parts of the pipeline take their transitions through the engine, and how an existing record is brought to a table state.

## Taking a transition

`apply_transition` takes a contribution record and a verb of the table. It never performs a remote effect; it returns the new record and the event to persist, or the reason the transition is refused.

1. **Resolve the verb.** The record's state is `phase.activity`. The verb is a row of that state, a rule row, or a setter or clearer of the `paused` flag. A verb that does not leave the state is an error of the caller.
2. **Apply the mask of the flag.** While `paused` is set, a controller tick is refused, a Result takes `record_late_effect` only, and of the commands only `resume`, `park`, `refresh`, `retract-pr`, `abandon`, and `resolve-operation` apply.
3. **Evaluate every guard.** Each guard text of the table is registered once in `engine.PREDICATES` under a name. A guard is one of two kinds:
   - a *record* predicate is decided by the engine from the record, the policy, the time, and the command: the exact revision, the candidate head, the pending human action, quiescence, staleness of a stop, the reminder interval, and the budgets;
   - a *fact* is something only the caller can observe, such as a CI verdict, an operation receipt, or a free worker slot. The caller asserts the fact by its name. A fact that is not asserted does not hold.

   A transition is refused unless every guard is established. A budget remains while its count is below its limit from `policy.json`; a budget whose count lives outside the record (dispatcher runs, an operation's attempts) is refused until the caller supplies the count. The rerun counts of the current head are in the record, in `budget.reruns` (`head_sha`, `flaky`, `interrupted`); counts recorded for another head, or no counts at all, are counts of zero for the current head. Whether a failing CI job is recorded for the head is decided from the evidence `last_ci_evidence_id` names, which the caller supplies from the store (`ci_evidence.failing_job`: the first executed blocking job of the head that did not succeed); passing evidence, evidence of another head, and review evidence name none, and without one `repair_attempts_cluster` is neither charged nor exhausted. When the record names evidence the caller does not supply, every guard that reads the failing job is refused. The controller supplies it from the store, from which evidence is never removed, so a record in a state that allocates Work whose `last_ci_evidence_id` names evidence the store does not hold is an unexpected error of that contribution: the controller takes `pause_on_processing_error` (see [Errors while a tick runs](#errors-while-a-tick-runs)) instead of deferring it silently. Two facts that state the opposite of each other, such as a CI failure on the candidate head and its absence, are refused together.
4. **Apply the effects of the row.** The engine sets and clears exactly the fields the row names. The caller supplies the value of a field the engine cannot derive; a value for a field the row does not set is an error. The requested Work is kept, cleared, or replaced as the row states. Some values are always derived by the engine: Work that a row allocates for a patch or a revision carries the pending feedback ids present at that moment (`active_work.feedback_evidence_ids`), a fresh Work graph and a Result held for a publish retry carry the same ids, and a row that records a new head starts `budget.reruns` again for that head. A row may clear a part of a field: a published Result clears exactly the feedback ids its Work carried. A generation bump resets every mutable lifecycle field and keeps identity and replay fences.
5. **Charge and restart budgets.** Each budget in the row's charges is counted. A resume starts again the one counter named in `pending_human_action.counter`, and no other.
6. **Enforce the revision rule.** The revision changes exactly when the row states a bump.
7. **Emit the event.** The event kind is the verb. Its `details` record the source and target state, the budgets charged, the counter started again with its count and the acting human, and what the caller adds (for the handoff entry, the exact head and revision).

`engine.apply_command` resolves an `/upstreaming` command to the one verb whose guards hold in the record's state and takes it. A `resume` of a paused contribution clears the flag first: it takes `resume_pause`, or `resume_stale` after a long stop in provisioning or development.

## What the pipeline takes through the engine

`engine.PIPELINE_VERBS` lists the verbs that pipeline code takes through the engine; `engine.UNWIRED_VERBS` is every other verb of the table. The two sets partition the table, and a test fails when a verb is in neither or in both. `uv run --locked python -m engine` prints both sets, the number of record and fact guards, and the remaining entries of the compatibility shim.

Every state of the `handoff` phase is written by the engine only. The stop flag, its commands, and the reminders are taken by the engine in every phase, for a record in a state of the table:

| Verb | Taken by | When |
|:-----|:---------|:-----|
| `nominate_for_handoff` | CI observation | Every required CI job executed and passed on the exact head, with no requested Work and no unprocessed actionable feedback. The contribution enters `handoff.awaiting_submission` with the pending action `submit_upstream`. |
| `return_for_repair` | CI observation | An executed blocking failure on the candidate head. The evidence of the failure is recorded as `last_ci_evidence_id`. |
| `record_actionable_review` | CI observation | Authorized actionable review feedback on the development PR while CI has not failed on the candidate head. The contribution returns to `development.fix_needed` with one revision bump, the feedback is recorded in `pending_feedback_evidence_ids`, and the CI evidence of the candidate head is kept. |
| `record_explanation` | CI observation | An authorized explanation-only comment on the development PR. The contribution stays in `handoff.awaiting_submission` with its revision, candidate head, and CI evidence; the tracking-issue entry quotes the comment. No Work is requested and nothing is answered automatically. |
| `record_head_change` | CI observation | The observed fork head differs from the candidate head. |
| `submit_upstream`, `retract_submission` | Command handling | See [Requesting upstream submission](#requesting-upstream-submission) and [The handoff operations](#the-handoff-operations). |
| `confirm_clean_head`, `retry_debug_cleanup`, `exhaust_debug_cleanup_retries`, `confirm_internal_pr_closed`, `retry_internal_pr_closure`, `exhaust_internal_pr_retries`, `confirm_submission_package`, `retry_submission_comment`, `exhaust_comment_retries` | Reconciler and controller, through the operation | The verified receipt of an attempt of a handoff operation, read back independently; the retry and exhaustion rows also when the controller closes an attempt whose launches are spent without a receipt. See [The handoff operations](#the-handoff-operations). `return_for_repair` is fired as well by a cleanup that cannot produce an admissible clean head or whose exact-head lease conflicts. |
| `accept_clean_head_ci` | CI observation | Every required CI job executed and passed on the clean head in `handoff.waiting_ci`; a blocking failure there, or a fork head that is not the clean head, takes `return_for_repair`. |
| `verify_upstream_pr`, `record_audit_observation` | Upstream PR observation | In the locked `handoff.waiting_upstream_pr`: exactly one upstream PR that satisfies every filter is verified; anything else that does not match is recorded once for audit. |
| `pause`, `resume_pause`, `resume_stale`, `park_contribution`, `refresh_investigation`, `restart_generation`, `abandon` | Command handling | Every command of the table (`pause`, `resume`, `park`, `refresh`, `abandon`, `submit-upstream`, `retract-pr`) on a record in a state of the table, in every phase; in the handoff phase every command. See [Stop and resume](#stop-and-resume) and [Commands](#commands). A command no row of the state takes is rejected with the state it does not apply in and the states it applies in. |
| `withdraw_request` | Controller | The intake label was removed from the tracking issue of a request in `queued.idle` with no Work, no dossier, and no fork. The contribution is closed with `terminal_reason` `withdrawn`. |
| `resume_investigation`, `resume_provisioning`, `resume_ci`, `resume_development`, `resume_debug_cleanup`, `resume_internal_pr_closure`, `resume_submission_preparation`, `resume_upstream_review`, `resume_upstream_revision`, `resume_release_observation` | Command handling | `/upstreaming resume` in a human wait of the table, guarded by the pending human action; each starts again the one counter that `pending_human_action.counter` names. |
| `record_reminder` | Controller | A pending human step in a state of kind `gate`, `human_wait`, or `locked`, once `human_sla_business_days.reminder` business days have passed since the request or the last reminder. See [Reminders](#reminders). |
| `pause_on_identity_change` | Controller | The observed upstream repository identity differs from the recorded one. |
| `pause_on_processing_error` | Reconciler | An unexpected error while the tick processed the contribution, see [Errors while a tick runs](#errors-while-a-tick-runs). The caller states `paused_reason`; the engine refuses the verb without one. |
| `begin_investigation`, `dispatch_investigation` | Controller | Investigation Work is due and a worker slot is free. A request in `queued.idle` takes `begin_investigation` in the tick that allocates its Work, then `dispatch_investigation`. See [Work identity and budgets](#work-identity-and-budgets). |
| `request_patch`, `request_repair` | Controller | Development Work is due in `development.fix_needed`, under write admission, with a worker slot and capacity: `request_repair` when a failing CI job is recorded for the head, `request_patch` otherwise. |
| `request_upstream_revision`, `request_review_revision` | Controller | Revision Work is due in `upstream_review.fix_needed`: with a failing CI job, or driven by review feedback only. |
| `exhaust_work_total`, `refresh_on_exhaustion`, `exhaust_repair_attempts` | Controller | A budget of the Work that is due is exhausted; taken without a worker slot, in place of the allocation. |
| `allocate_work_retry`, `exhaust_work_recovery` | Reconciler | The requested Work graph is terminal (completed or cancelled in the queue) without a folded Result. |
| `admit_development`, `readmit_development`, `park_investigation`, `close_investigation` | Reconciler | The verified dossier of the requested investigation decides. See [The dossier decides](#the-dossier-decides). |
| `reject_dossier`, `exhaust_investigation_recovery` | Reconciler | An investigation Result has no dossier, a dossier whose decision is none of develop, park, and close, or a patch scope that is incomplete where the admission checks it. |
| `start_operation` | Controller | A planned fork provisioning operation of a record in `provisioning.idle`, with capacity, within `operation_launch`. See [Remote operations](#remote-operations). |
| `require_provisioning_intervention` | Controller | The planned provisioning operation is still without a receipt after its last launch and `operation_launch` is exhausted, or a human resolved the operation `failed`. |
| `confirm_provisioning` | Reconciler, through the operation | The provisioning operation is confirmed by its receipt and the exact readback of the fork and `riscv64`. |

A record in a state that only the compatibility layer writes (`compat.LEGACY_STATES`, for example `development.idle` or `development.waiting_capacity`) has no row to take. For such a record the three verbs that set `paused` are written by `compat.legacy_pause` with the same fields (`paused_at`, `paused_by`, `paused_reason`) and the same clears (the requested Work and the pending publication); the activity that held the Work or the publication becomes `idle`. `/upstreaming pause`, `resume`, and `park` on such a record are applied by `compat.apply_command`. Every other command of the table (`refresh`, `abandon`, `submit-upstream`, `retract-pr`) applies in states of the table only and is rejected there with the states it applies in.

At the submission gate the collector reads the review comments and CI on the candidate head in one observation and then takes at most one of the two rows that leave the gate for repair. Their guards are exclusive: when CI failed on the candidate head, `return_for_repair` applies and the review comments are left unread, so they are recorded when the development PR is observed again; otherwise `record_actionable_review` applies. The ids of the comments a row recorded are added to the replay fences of the record in the same write.

A worker Result is folded only when it matches the Work identity: the contribution is in a working phase (`investigation`, `provisioning`, `development`, or `upstream_review`), it is not paused, the generation and revision the Result's task names are the record's, and the Result answers the requested Work (its Work id, `active_work.dedupe_key`) or the trusted publication of the same worker run (a publish-only retry). Each phase requests its own Work only (investigation Work in `investigation`, patch Work in `development`, revision Work in `upstream_review`), so a Result of Work of another phase is late as well; a dossier is folded in `investigation` only, and a Result held for a publish retry in `development` and `upstream_review` only. `park` and `pause` clear the requested Work and the pending publication without a revision bump, so an equal revision alone is not a match. Every other Result takes `record_late_effect`, in every state: a Result of another generation, of another revision, of other Work, a Result that arrives when the record holds no requested Work, a Result for a record that waits for a human, and an older publication artifact of the same Work once the newest is folded. The reconciler marks the publication artifact as processed and appends one event with the Result's status, task generation, revision, and Work id, and nothing else in the record changes. An artifact that is already marked as processed is not recorded again, so each late Result is recorded once.

A stop invalidates what is held for publication. `park_contribution`, `abandon`, the verbs that set `paused`, and every other transition that ends the generation clear `pending_publication` together with the requested Work; `python -m lifecycle check` verifies it. `proposal_artifact` only ever holds a published Result, which a stop keeps: no patch is held without a write to the provisioned fork, and a worker outcome `proposal`, which no publisher produces any more, is rejected in trusted validation. Nothing is therefore published after a stop: the resume finds nothing held and requests fresh Work. The legacy `park` and `pause` paths in `compat.py` do the same. A record stopped before stops cleared these fields is rewritten by the [flag migration](#flag-migration). The publication Result that arrives afterwards takes `record_late_effect`. No publication is launched for a paused record: the publish-only retry skips it. A stop therefore never keeps `/upstreaming resume` from applying because of a publication that was pending when it was taken.

A branch that the worker run already pushed is recorded by head observation, which reads the fork branch in the `development` and `upstream_review` phases and in `handoff.awaiting_submission`, also while the record is paused: the observed head replaces the recorded one and the revision is bumped. While the record holds requested Work, or a Result held for a publish retry that is not exhausted, a changed head is not recorded: the table has no head row in an `agent_running` state, so the Result is folded at the revision its Work was requested for, and the head it published is recorded by the observation after it. A record that waits for a human in a state of the table that has no observation row (a state of kind `human_wait` with a recorded pending action, such as `development.waiting_human` or `upstream_review.waiting_human`) is not observed either: its head, CI, and review comments are read again once a resume, `park`, or `refresh` has left the wait, so observation never changes its state, revision, evidence, or pending action. A parked or closed record is not observed. Its recorded head stays as it was, and the pushed commit stays on the fork branch; a resume from `parked.idle` begins a new generation that records the fork and its head again at provisioning. The verb is taken through `compat.record_late_effect`, which uses the engine for a table state and writes the same event for a state of `LEGACY_STATES`.

In the other phases the controller, the reconciler, and the collector still decide state changes in their own code. They write the lifecycle fields through `compat.py`, a compatibility shim with two registries: `LEGACY_SITES` names each call site that still writes state outside the table, and `LEGACY_STATES` lists the states those call sites produce that the table does not have (for example `development.idle` and the capacity waits). The shim refuses any write that enters the handoff phase, changes a record that is in it, changes the phase or activity of a record that waits for a human in a state of the table (`compat.waits_for_human`: a state of kind `gate` or `locked`, or of kind `human_wait` with a recorded pending action, which only the rows the table lists for it leave), or leaves a record in a state that is in neither the table nor `LEGACY_STATES`. The legacy paths that would leave such a wait skip the record instead: the CI and review observation and the development PR and CI bootstrap plans. Every state of the `queued`, `investigation`, and `provisioning` phases is a state of the table, and those phases are written by the engine only: the dossier decides by the table's rows (see [The dossier decides](#the-dossier-decides)), and provisioning is planned, launched, and confirmed from `provisioning.idle` (see [Remote operations](#remote-operations)). The shapes written before the table are rewritten by the [provisioning migration](#provisioning-migration). No module other than `engine.py` and `compat.py` assigns a lifecycle field; a test scans the source for such assignments.

The final check before a fork write (`lifecycle.publication_check`) reads the table for a record in a handoff state: an automated writer may write the fork only in a handoff state the table marks as writing. At the submission gate it may not.

State validation applies the same rule to committed records: a contribution is in a table state or in a state of `LEGACY_STATES`, and a handoff wait carries a pending action with which the table enters that wait.

## Work identity and budgets

Work is allocated by the rows of the table, and every allocation is an event whose kind is the verb. The controller considers a contribution in `investigation.idle` (or `queued.idle`, which first takes `begin_investigation`), `development.fix_needed`, or `upstream_review.fix_needed` that is not paused and holds no Work, pending publication, or open operation. When the guard of an exhaustion row of that state holds, the row is taken in place of the allocation, without a worker slot. Otherwise the allocation row whose guards hold is taken once a worker slot (and, for write Work, capacity) is free; while none is free the contribution is reported as deferred and nothing changes.

The Work identity is the generation, the revision, and the Work id. The Work id is `active_work.dedupe_key`, `<contribution>@g<generation>.r<revision>:<task>`, and the queue graph is derived from it, so Work of another generation or revision is another graph and a terminal graph is never submitted again. `active_work.generation` records the generation. The Work payload (`task.json`) carries `generation` and `dedupe_key` beside `revision`, and the reconciler and the publisher's final check (`lifecycle.publication_check`) compare all three with the record: the publisher writes the fork only for the record's generation and revision, and only while the record holds that Work, or a Result held for a publish retry. A payload written before the identity carried the generation names neither `generation` nor `dedupe_key`; it is generation 0, its Work is recognized by its revision, and its Result can never be folded into a later generation. Requested Work recorded without `active_work.generation` is generation 0 in the same way, and a replay of it submits the payload it was first submitted with, without the new fields.

The task the Work asks a worker for follows the row: `dispatch_investigation` asks for an investigation, `request_repair` for a repair of the failing CI job, `request_patch` for a patch, or for a repair when feedback is pending for it to answer, and the two upstream rows for a revision. Work that a row allocates carries the pending feedback ids present at allocation (`active_work.feedback_evidence_ids`), and the payload passes exactly those ids to the worker in `feedback_evidence_ids`; feedback recorded later is not carried. Its reason ids are the carried feedback, then the CI evidence and the dossier.

The budgets of `BUDGETS` are counted where the table charges them:

| Budget | Count | Charged by | Exhausted by |
|:-------|:------|:-----------|:-------------|
| `work_total` | `budget.attempts_used`, per generation | every allocation, once: `dispatch_investigation`, `request_patch`, `request_repair`, the upstream revision rows, `allocate_work_retry` | `exhaust_work_total` (a human wait whose pending action names `work_total`), `exhaust_work_recovery` |
| `work_recovery` | `active_work.attempt` | `allocate_work_retry` | `exhaust_work_recovery` (names `work_total` when that is exhausted, `work_recovery` otherwise) |
| `investigation_attempts` | `budget.investigation_attempts` | `reject_dossier` | `exhaust_investigation_recovery` (names `investigation_attempts`) |
| `repair_attempts_cluster` | `budget.clusters[<failing CI job>]` | `request_repair` and `request_upstream_revision`, at allocation, for the failing CI job of the head | `refresh_on_exhaustion` once per generation, then `exhaust_repair_attempts` (parks); in upstream review a human wait that names the counter |
| `no_progress` | `budget.no_progress_streak` | every rejected or blocked worker outcome; started again by a published patch and by `refresh_on_exhaustion` | as `repair_attempts_cluster` |
| `operation_launch` | `external_ids.launch_attempts` of the planned provisioning operation | `start_operation` | `require_provisioning_intervention` (names `operation_launch`) |

A rejected or blocked outcome charges nothing else: `work_total` and the cluster are charged when the next Work is allocated. A resume from a human wait an exhausted budget caused starts again exactly the counter the pending action names. The requested Work of the first patch in `development.idle`, a state the table does not have, is still selected by the controller's own code; it carries the pending feedback and charges `work_total` like `request_patch`, and is not requested while `work_total` or `no_progress` is exhausted.

## The dossier decides

An investigation Result is folded in `investigation.agent_running` only, through the table's rows. The dossier is recorded as evidence first; then exactly one row is taken:

| Dossier | Row | Leads to |
|:--------|:----|:---------|
| None, or a decision that is none of develop, park, and close | `reject_dossier`, or `exhaust_investigation_recovery` once `investigation_attempts` is spent | `investigation.idle` at a new revision (a new investigation is due), or `investigation.waiting_human` |
| Develop, and a refresh recorded that began with the fork and development PR the record still holds | `readmit_development` | `development.fix_needed` on the recorded fork, at a new revision |
| Develop otherwise, with a complete patch scope | `admit_development` | `provisioning.idle` at a new revision; the fork is provisioned before any patch Work |
| Develop otherwise, with an incomplete patch scope | `reject_dossier` (or `exhaust_investigation_recovery`) | as for an invalid dossier |
| Park, with a recorded `admission_override` that names a class and paths | `admit_development`, or `readmit_development` as for develop | as for develop, with the override's class and paths |
| Park, with an override that names no class or no paths | `reject_dossier` (or `exhaust_investigation_recovery`) | as for an invalid dossier |
| Park, without an override | `park_investigation` | `parked.idle`, with the dossier's rationale as `parked_reason`; the generation ends without a revision bump |
| Close | `close_investigation` | `closed.idle` with `terminal_reason` `no_work`; a recorded override is not applied, and the event and the tracking-issue entry say so |

The patch scope is a guard of `admit_development` (the fact "the verified dossier names `scope_paths`, and `scope_coverage_complete` holds"): the reconciler establishes it when the admitted scope has an admission class, names 1 to 32 safe scope paths, and matches every path the dossier's `path_coverage` lists as possibly needing a riscv64 change (`dossier_gate.scope_problem`). For an override the paths are the human's scope: the class and the paths are required, and the coverage is recorded but does not refuse it. `readmit_development` has no scope guard. Both admissions record the dossier, the admission class, and the scope paths, clear the refresh and the override, and record beside the row in `scope_coverage_complete` whether the coverage was complete for the admitted scope; repair Work reads it. A rejected dossier keeps the override and the refresh for the next investigation.

## Errors while a tick runs

An error in one contribution does not stop the controller tick. Every step of a tick that works on one contribution runs inside `isolation.contribution(contribution_id, step)`: the confirmation of a development PR or provisioning receipt, the fold of a worker Result, the Work deadline, dispatch recovery, the recovery of recorded review feedback, the observation of the fork branch and CI, the canary recovery, the intake-label check, the source identity check, the application of a command, the discovery alerts of an operation, the provisioning and development PR plans, Work selection, the publish-only retry, and the check of the operations the tick stages. An exception that leaves such a block is attributed to that contribution and classified by its class:

| Exception | Class | What the tick does |
|:----------|:------|:-------------------|
| `collect.CollectError`, `http.client.HTTPException`, `OSError` | Collector failure: GitHub could not be read for the contribution. | The contribution is skipped for the tick: no state change, no pause. The report lists it under `skipped_contributions` with the step, the error class, and the message. |
| Every other exception, including `lifecycle.TransitionError` and `schemas.SchemaError` raised outside the application of a command | Unexpected error (`isolation.ProcessingError`). | Everything staged for the contribution is discarded, none of its effects is requested, the other contributions are processed, and the contribution takes `pause_on_processing_error`. |

Every GitHub read of the collector goes through `collect.Api`, which turns each failure to read a response into `collect.CollectError` with a bounded message: an HTTP error status, an error of the HTTP layer (`http.client.HTTPException`, for example `IncompleteRead` or `RemoteDisconnected`), an error of the transport (`OSError`, which covers `urllib.error.URLError`, TLS errors such as `ssl.SSLEOFError`, socket timeouts, and reset connections), and a response body that is not JSON (`json.JSONDecodeError`, `UnicodeDecodeError`), for example one cut off in transit. The body itself is never quoted. Only the read and the decoding are converted: a `KeyError`, `TypeError`, or `ValueError` raised while a well-formed response is used is an unexpected error. The HTTP-layer and transport classes are also in the collector class above, as a safety net for a read that does not go through `collect.Api`.

Expected rejections never reach the block and pause nothing, as before: a `TransitionError` raised while a command is applied and a command the table or the legacy path refuses are recorded as a rejected command with the reason, a command that fails authentication or the grammar is recorded as rejected, and an intake form that raises `intake.IntakeError` is reported as invalid. A malformed development PR receipt (`KeyError`, `ValueError`) is ignored until a valid one arrives.

Staging is all or nothing per contribution. `reconcile.tick` computes the tick on a copy of the committed state. When a contribution fails, the tick is computed again from the committed state with that contribution left out: no step processes it, so no record, event, operation, evidence, or command receipt is staged for it and no Work, dispatcher launch, provisioning, development PR, publication, or publish retry is requested for it. It still counts as an existing contribution for the others: it keeps its worker slot and its upstream. A command posted on its tracking issue, `resume` included, is left unconsumed and gets no reply: it is read again by every tick until one processes the contribution, and is applied or rejected then. A request that would become a new contribution in this tick is deferred and read again by the next tick. Before the result is returned, the tick verifies that nothing staged and nothing requested names the contribution, and stops otherwise.

After an unexpected error the controller takes `pause_on_processing_error` on the record as it was committed before the tick, through the engine for a record in a state of the table and through `compat.legacy_pause` for a record in a legacy state. The verb sets `paused` with `paused_by` `controller` and `paused_at`, clears the requested Work and the pending publication, and keeps the revision; from an `agent_running` state it leads to the state in which the Work is due again; `paused_reason` is `processing_error: <error class>: <message> (step <step>, run <run id>)`. The record and the event are the only things the tick stages for the contribution. The event carries the step, the error class, the message, and the run id in its `details`; the status ledger posts them on the tracking issue with the statement that everything else of that run was discarded. The report lists the error under `controller.alerts` and, with its fields, under `processing_errors`. `python -m reconcile` still exits with status 0, since the tick goes on, and prints one GitHub Actions `::error::` annotation per processing error, built from the same bounded text and escaped as the workflow command format requires, so that a pipeline bug charged to a contribution is visible on the workflow run. `/upstreaming resume` at the current revision clears the pause and bumps the revision.

The error text is bounded and sanitized before it is stored or posted (`isolation.clean_text`): the text normalization of the state store is applied, everything that matches a credential pattern of the store is replaced, control characters and line breaks become one space, and the message is cut to 300 characters. The traceback is written to the log of the workflow run only.

A contribution that is already stopped is not paused again. The verb is guarded by `not paused`, like every verb that sets the flag, so an error in a contribution that is paused, by a human or by the controller, changes nothing and appends no event, whatever the error is; the same holds for a terminal contribution. Observations still fold while a contribution is paused, so an error that persists is met again by every tick: each tick leaves the contribution out, lists the error in its report, and writes nothing. The next error after a resume pauses it again. An error in a contribution that is already stopped, and in a contribution that the same tick would have created from an intake request, which has no committed record to pause, is reported only in the tick report (`controller.alerts` and `processing_errors`) and in the workflow annotation: no event is appended and nothing is posted on the tracking issue.

Stop commands are applied before any Work is selected. When applying `pause` or `park` fails with an unexpected error, the contribution is errored like in any other step: the tick is computed again without it, so nothing is launched for it, and it is paused by the controller.

The whole tick stops, nothing is committed, and no contribution is paused when:

- unexpected errors occur in two or more contributions in one tick. Collector failures do not count: any number of contributions can be skipped;
- `pause_on_processing_error` cannot be applied to the errored contribution, for example because its record is in no admitted state;
- an exception is raised outside every block: the state load and the validation of the store, the policy, the text and record migrations, the collection of the tick's inputs (intake issues, command comments, tracking issues, source snapshots), command authentication, the intake of a request that is not a contribution yet, a command on an issue that tracks no contribution, the staging of the result into the store, and the state commit.

`python -m reconcile` exits with status 1 and a message that names the contributions and their errors in the first two cases, and with the error itself in the third, as before. The record migration is in the third group although it works on one record: a record that awaits the migration is not in a state of the table, so no row could pause it, and the state load refuses a handoff record in any other shape.

## Stop and resume

`/upstreaming pause`, `resume`, `park`, `refresh`, and `abandon` on a record in a state of the table are applied by `engine.apply_command`, in every phase; the controller resolves the one verb whose guards hold and refuses the command with the guard that does not.

- `pause` sets `paused` with `paused_by` `human`, `paused_at`, and the optional reason in `paused_reason`. It clears the requested Work and the pending publication, and keeps the revision. In an `agent_running` state it leads to the state in which the Work is due again (`investigation.idle`, `development.fix_needed`, `upstream_review.fix_needed`), so the resume requests fresh Work. A Result of the stopped Work takes `record_late_effect`. While paused no Work is requested, no budget is charged, no publication or publish retry is launched, and no reminder is posted.
- `resume` on a paused record clears the flag and bumps the revision (`resume_pause`). After a stop longer than `refresh.stale_after_days` days in `provisioning` or `development`, counted from the older of `paused_at` and `pending_human_action.requested_at`, it takes `resume_stale` instead: a complete investigation runs again from `queued.idle` while the fork and development PR are kept, and the refresh records the phase, head, and development PR it began from (no head or PR when there is no fork yet). A refreshed dossier returns to development on the recorded fork only when the refresh began with that fork and its development PR; otherwise development is admitted afresh. In the handoff phase the resume never refreshes: the candidate head, `handoff_request`, and the CI evidence are kept, and `submit-upstream` then names the new revision and the same head.
- `resume` in a human wait that is not paused takes the resume of that wait that its pending human action selects; on a paused human wait the first `resume` clears the flag only, and its tracking-issue entry names the pending action that remains, so a second `resume` leaves the wait.
- `resume` in `parked.idle` starts a new generation at `queued.idle` (`restart_generation`); it requires the exact revision, no open operation and no pending publication, and no open upstream PR. It accepts an optional admission class and paths, `/upstreaming resume revision=N class=<class> paths="<glob>,<glob>" reason="..."`, which are recorded with the reason as `admission_override`; the paths must be safe relative paths, as for a scope. The new generation still runs the complete investigation; a dossier that decides park then admits development with the override's class and paths, and a dossier that decides develop or close is applied as it decides (see [The dossier decides](#the-dossier-decides)). `class` and `paths` are refused by every other resume.
- `abandon` applies in `parked.idle` only, with a reason and at any revision, also while paused. It leads to `closed.idle` with `terminal_reason` `abandoned`; the reason the human gave is kept in the event. A closed contribution is final: no command resumes it. Elsewhere the command is rejected with the statement that it applies in `parked.idle`; park first.
- `refresh` applies in the states whose rows take it: `development.fix_needed`, `development.waiting_ci`, `development.waiting_human`, `handoff.awaiting_submission`, and `handoff.waiting_upstream_pr`, with the exact revision, no requested Work, no open operation or pending publication, and a recorded development PR and head; it is accepted while paused. In `handoff.waiting_upstream_pr` it names the exact clean head, `/upstreaming refresh revision=N head=<clean head> reason="..."`; elsewhere a `head` is refused.
- `park` takes `park_contribution` from every state the rule covers, with a reason and with no open operation; it is accepted while paused. It does not apply in `handoff.waiting_upstream_pr`, `release_wait.*`, and the `upstream_review` states other than `upstream_review.waiting_human`.

A stop command is honored at a stale revision; every other command names the current revision. The reply to a `pause` of a paused contribution says it is already paused, and the reply to a `resume` of a contribution that is neither paused nor waiting says it is not paused.

`/upstreaming block` is retired: the lifecycle has one stop flag, `paused`, with its reason and who set it. The command is rejected with a message that names `pause`. The controller sets the same flag when the upstream repository identity changes (`pause_on_identity_change`, `paused_by` `controller`, `paused_reason` `upstream_identity_changed`) and after a processing error.

## Commands

The grammar (`commands.GRAMMAR`) has the commands of the table and two intake commands:

| Command | Applies in |
|:--------|:-----------|
| `pause`, `resume`, `park`, `refresh`, `abandon`, `submit-upstream`, `retract-pr` | The states whose rows, or the `paused` flag, take them; see [Stop and resume](#stop-and-resume) and [Requesting upstream submission](#requesting-upstream-submission). |
| `authorize-followup` | A merged contribution (`release_wait`, `released`); it records a follow-up request. Outside the table. |
| `resolve-operation` | An operation, by its id; see the operation table. Outside the table. |

The retired commands are rejected with a message that names their replacement, and the reply on the tracking issue shows the replacement's form:

| Retired | Replacement |
|:--------|:------------|
| `request-handoff`, `approve-handoff`, `register-upstream-pr` | `submit-upstream` at the submission gate; the upstream PR is verified by observation. |
| `block` | `pause`, with a reason. |
| `approve-scope` | None needed: the verified dossier decides admission and provisioning starts automatically. A parked contribution resumes from `parked.idle`, with `class` and `paths` as an admission override. |
| `provision` | None needed: the fork is provisioned automatically. In `provisioning.waiting_human`, correct the prerequisite, then `resume`; the resume states that the prerequisite is corrected. |
| `restart` | `resume` from `parked.idle`; `closed.idle` is final. |
| `clarify` | `resume` in the human wait once the cause is corrected. |
| `revise-scope` | `refresh`, or `resume` from `parked.idle` with `class` and `paths`. |
| `force-develop` | `resume` from `parked.idle` with `class` and `paths`: the admission override that a dossier decision park then applies. |

The tracking-issue entries offer, under "Possible next commands", the commands the table takes in the contribution's state (in `parked.idle` also the resume with an admission override, prefilled with the recorded class and paths); in a state that only the compatibility layer writes they offer `pause`, `park`, and `resume`. A retired command is never offered.

## Reminders

Every tick, the controller takes `record_reminder` for each record in a state of kind `gate`, `human_wait`, or `locked` whose pending human step was requested, or last reminded, at least `human_sla_business_days.reminder` business days ago (Monday to Friday, counted in whole days from the exact time of the request or of the last reminder). The row sets `pending_human_action.reminded_at` and nothing else; the revision is kept. While the record is paused no reminder is posted. The event carries the pending action and, for `handoff.awaiting_submission`, the revision and candidate head; its tracking-issue entry repeats the exact `submit-upstream` command for them. In `handoff.waiting_upstream_pr` the event also carries the link to the posted submission package, the human who requested the submission, and what the observation of the same tick found that does not match: the entry mentions that human, links the package, names the exact clean head, and either states that the branch still resolves to that head or states the mismatch and offers the exact `retract-pr` command instead of the package.

## Requesting upstream submission

`handoff.awaiting_submission` is the gate at which a human requests upstream submission. The tracking-issue entry for `nominate_for_handoff` states the exact head on which CI passed and shows the command to post:

```text
/upstreaming submit-upstream revision=N head=<full head SHA>
```

The grammar requires the revision and the full 40-character head. The engine accepts the command exactly as the table defines `submit_upstream`: the revision is the current one, the head is the candidate head recorded in `handoff_request` and still the fork head, and the contribution is not paused. A resume from a pause bumps the revision and keeps the candidate head, so the command then names the new revision and the same head. Outside the handoff phase the command is rejected with a reason that names the state the contribution is in and `handoff.awaiting_submission`, the state the command applies in.

`submit_upstream` leads to `handoff.cleaning_debug` and authorizes the deterministic handoff preparation described in [The handoff operations](#the-handoff-operations). It records `handoff_approval` (the command, its actor, the exact revision and head, and the CI and dossier evidence it saw) and the first debug cleanup operation as `active_operation_id`; the operation is launched in the same tick. No second command is needed until the upstream PR is open.

The commands `request-handoff`, `approve-handoff`, and `register-upstream-pr` are retired. Each is rejected with a message that names `submit-upstream`.

`/upstreaming retract-pr revision=N head=<clean head>` withdraws the submission package in `handoff.waiting_upstream_pr` (`retract_submission`, to `development.fix_needed`, with the exact revision, the exact clean head, and quiescence). The row reads no reason, so the command takes none. It bumps the revision, clears the pending action, `handoff_request`, and `handoff_approval`, so reminders stop, and unlocks branch work. No upstream resource is changed.

## The handoff operations

Three handoff states each run one remote operation through the trusted workflow `handoff.yml`. No model runs there and no credential can write upstream; the authorized human opens the upstream PR under their own account.

| State | Operation kind | Effect and readback | Token |
|:------|:---------------|:--------------------|:------|
| `handoff.cleaning_debug` | `debug_cleanup` | The admitted series from the base to the approved head is rebuilt in a fresh repository without its `{debug}` commits (the App identity and the original dates, so the same series gives the same clean head), validated like a publication (scope, workflow safety, no debug commit), and pushed with `--force-with-lease=refs/heads/riscv64:<approved head>`; the branch is read back. With no debug commit nothing is written: the branch is read back at the approved head, which becomes the clean head. | destination App, the one fork, `contents: write`, `workflows: write` |
| `handoff.closing_internal_pr` | `internal_pr_closure` | The exact recorded development PR (its number, `riscv64` of the fork at the clean head, the frozen base, the development marker) is closed and read back closed. | destination App, the one fork, `pull-requests: write` |
| `handoff.preparing_submission` | `submission_comment` | One App-authored comment on the tracking issue carries the submission package, found again by its hidden marker (contribution, approved revision, clean head) so it is posted once; it is read back. | control App, `porting-agent`, `issues: write` |

Each job of the workflow checks out the state at the head of `main` when it starts, not the commit the run was dispatched at, and before it mints its token checks that this commit is still the head of `main` (`handoff.current_state`) and that the state still runs the operation, of the job's kind (`python -m handoff check`); a refusal there is the job's receipt and its later steps do not run, so a queued or re-run job never acts on state that `main` has left. Before any effect the workflow checks the operation against that state again (`handoff.load_request`): the operation is `planned` and of the kind of the record's state, the record names it as its active operation and is not paused, the policy permits the write, and the target is the recorded fork and `riscv64`, the approved head or the recorded clean head, the recorded development PR, or the tracking issue. A refused check writes nothing and its receipt is not folded. Every other failure of an attempt is reported by a receipt: `invalid` for a cleanup that cannot produce an admissible clean head (a conflict once the debug commits are removed, a merge, nothing left, a validation problem) or whose lease conflicts (the branch moved), `infrastructure` for everything else.

The controller records the intent of each attempt and launches it like the other workflow operations: counted on the operation (`operation_launch`), again after `LAUNCH_INTERVAL_SECONDS` without a receipt, and not while the contribution is paused. Each attempt is its own operation of the same intent: its reason ids are the approval, `attempt:<n>`, and `count:<k>`, the count of the budget of the state (`debug_cleanup`, `internal_pr_closure`, `submission_comment`) at that attempt. The reconciler folds a receipt only for the open operation the record runs, reads back what it reports (the branch and the clean series without debug commits; the PR closed at the clean head; the App-authored comment with its marker and digest), and fires the row bound in `recovery.FIRES`:

- a confirmed attempt fires `confirm_clean_head` (sets `clean_head_sha` and `fork.head_sha`, and starts the rerun counts for the clean head), `confirm_internal_pr_closed`, or `confirm_submission_package` (sets the pending action `open_upstream_pr` and `handoff_request`, whose `command_id` is the package operation);
- a failed attempt fires the retry row while the count is below the limit (`retries.infrastructure_per_head_failure` plus one attempts), and the next attempt is planned and launched in the same tick; at the limit it fires the exhaustion row to `handoff.waiting_human` with the pending action and the counter of the budget, and the next attempt counts from one after the resume;
- an `invalid` cleanup fires `return_for_repair` to `development.fix_needed`.

An attempt whose launches are spent without a receipt (a cancelled or timed-out run, a lost runner, a dispatch that failed) is a definite infrastructure failure: once the last allowed launch is `LAUNCH_INTERVAL_SECONDS` old and the contribution is not paused, the controller closes it as failed (`begin_effect`, `record_failure`, with `failure` `infrastructure` and `launches_spent`) and fires the same retry or exhaustion row as a failed receipt (`handoff.fail_attempt`); a retry plans and launches the next attempt in the same tick. Every handoff work state therefore ends in its next state or in `handoff.waiting_human`, whose resume starts the budget again. A receipt of the closed attempt that arrives later is not folded.

The readback of a rewritten clean head checks that each removed commit is a `{debug}` commit of the series from the base to the approved head, and that the clean series is that series without exactly the removed commits, in order and with the same messages. Git receives the fork token as configuration in the environment of the git process (`GIT_CONFIG_COUNT`), never on its command line; the publisher does the same.

`handoff.waiting_ci` is observed like development CI, on the clean head only: `accept_clean_head_ci` when every required job executed and passed (the evidence already recorded for that exact head counts), `return_for_repair` for a blocking failure or a fork head that is not the clean head. A run still in progress, a missing run, or a job that did not execute keeps the wait.

`handoff.waiting_upstream_pr` is locked: its exits are `verify_upstream_pr`, `retract_submission`, and `refresh_investigation` (with the exact clean head), and nothing else changes its state. The observation lists the open pull requests of the upstream repository whose head is `riscv64` of the fork owner and verifies exactly one that is open, against the upstream repository and the frozen base, from the fork's `riscv64` at the exact clean head, and authored by a human whose account id is in `command_authority.actor_ids`. It records `upstream_pr` with that author and enters `upstream_review.waiting_ci`. A fork head that is not the clean head, a candidate that fails a filter, or several matching candidates take `record_audit_observation` once per distinct observation (its key is in the event), and the next reminder states it. No Work, no publication, no write to the fork, and no automatic exit happens in the wait.

### The submission package

The package is derived from the committed record, the verified dossier, the CI evidence of the clean head, and the commits from the base to the clean head (`handoff.build_package`), so the same inputs give the same bytes:

- the title is the subject of the one commit, or `Build riscv64 wheels` for a series, at most 120 characters;
- the description is the body of the one commit (each commit with its body for a series), a `Testing` section with the number of jobs that executed and passed on the exact clean head and the riscv64 jobs among them, and the open issues of the upstream repository that the dossier records; at most 12000 characters;
- the prefilled link is `https://github.com/<upstream>/compare/<base>...<fork owner>:riscv64?quick_pull=1&title=<encoded>&body=<encoded>`, every value percent-encoded; when it would be longer than 8000 characters it carries the title only and the comment says to paste the description;
- a mention in the title or the description (`@user` or `@org/team` outside code) is put in a code span, so the upstream pull request notifies nobody, and the comment says so;
- the comment shows the upstream repository and base with its id, the source fork and branch with its id, the exact head, the changed files, the package operation and the dossier evidence, a warning to check that the compare form shows exactly that head (with the exact `retract-pr` command otherwise, and a note that after a pause and its resume the latest tracking-issue entry carries the current revision), the title and the full description outside the link, the contribution rules of the dossier, the admitted paths the series does not change, a reminder to apply a sign-off or a contributor agreement (left out when the rules state the signing policy), how to carry a pull request template over, what to do when the human's account cannot open the pull request (another human of the command authority opens it from the same package, never from another fork or branch), and the hidden marker;
- every untrusted value (the contribution rules, the admitted paths, the changed files, the commit messages, the CI job names) is in a code span with its backticks replaced or in a fenced block longer than any backtick run it contains, so none of it renders as a mention, a link, an image, or HTML; the only mention is the human who requested the submission. Untrusted text is also stripped of credential patterns and HTML comments.

The workflow posts the package only while `riscv64` still resolves to the clean head; a moved branch fails the attempt (`retry_submission_comment`, then `exhaust_comment_retries`). A package already posted is found by its marker and read back.

## Record migration

Before the lifecycle table, passing CI moved a contribution to the phase `handoff` with the activity `waiting_human` and the pending action `approve_handoff`. The table has no such wait. The controller rewrites such a record itself, before it does anything else in a tick:

- The migration is bound to one exact shape: the old approval wait with no requested Work, no open operation, no pending publication, no approval, no upstream PR, not paused, no pending actionable feedback, the candidate head equal to the fork head, the request for the current revision, and CI evidence that states that CI passed on that head. A handoff record in any other shape that is not a table state has no migration: the controller stops with an error and writes nothing.
- The new record is what `nominate_for_handoff` gives for the same CI evidence: `handoff.awaiting_submission`, the pending action `submit_upstream`, and no clean head (the clean head is produced by the debug cleanup). The revision, the fork, the development PR, the head, the dossier, the CI evidence, the budgets, and the replay fences are kept.
- The record and one appended event of kind `handoff_wait_migrated` are the only paths of that state commit; the tick requests no effect. The state publisher's final check accepts the commit and refuses any commit that writes the old shape again.
- The migration is idempotent: a migrated record is in a table state and is not selected again.

The tracking-issue entry for the migration event states that the fork, development PR, head, revision, and CI evidence are unchanged, that the earlier request to run `approve-handoff` no longer applies, and shows the `submit-upstream` command for the exact revision and head.

## Flag migration

Before the stop flag was merged, a `block` wrote `blocked_reason` and a human wait in place, a pause did not record `paused_at`, and a stop did not always clear what was held for publication. The controller rewrites such a record in the same way, in its own state-only commit with one appended event of kind `paused_flag_migrated` (cause `migration`, `paused-flag-merge`), before the ordinary tick:

- A `blocked_reason` becomes `paused`: `paused_by` `controller` and `paused_reason` `upstream_identity_changed` for the controller's block on a changed upstream identity, `paused_by` `human` and the block's reason otherwise. `blocked_reason` becomes null; the schema admits no other value. A block's human wait without a pending action becomes `idle`, as the old `resume` left it; with a pending action the wait is kept.
- A paused record without `paused_at` gets its `updated_at`, and one without `paused_by` gets `human`.
- A paused, parked, or terminal record loses the requested Work and the pending publication, as the table's stops clear them, and a proposal it holds without having published it.
- Nothing else changes. A blocked record in the handoff phase, or a block over a human wait that the table does not have, has no migration: the controller stops with an error and writes nothing. A record that needs none of this is not touched. The migration is idempotent.

## Terminal migration

Before the lifecycle table, `/upstreaming abandon` in any phase and the removal of the intake label wrote the terminal phase `abandoned`, and the schema also admitted `no_work`, `deferred`, and `already_released`. The table has one terminal phase for work that is not needed, `closed.idle`, with the reason in `terminal_reason`. The controller rewrites such a record in its own state-only commit with one appended event of kind `terminal_phase_migrated` (cause `migration`, `closed-terminal-phase`), before the ordinary tick:

- The phase becomes `closed` and the activity stays `idle`. `terminal_reason` becomes `withdrawn` for the removed intake label (the reason the controller recorded), `abandoned` for every other `abandoned` record, and the name of the old phase for the others. The text it held before is kept in the event.
- Nothing else changes: the revision, the generation, the flag, the evidence, and the replay fences are kept.
- A record with requested Work, an open operation, a pending publication, a held proposal, a text `blocked_reason`, or an activity other than `idle` has no migration: the controller stops with an error and writes nothing. The state publisher's final check refuses any commit that writes an old terminal phase. The migration is idempotent.

## Provisioning migration

Before the lifecycle table, provisioning waited in shapes the table does not have, and a patch could be proposed before the fork existed while destination writes were disabled. The controller rewrites such a record in its own state-only commit with one appended event of kind `provisioning_state_migrated` (cause `migration`, `provisioning-table-states`), before the ordinary tick:

- `provisioning.waiting_capacity`, a planned or launched fork intent, becomes `provisioning.idle`. The planned operation is kept and is launched by `start_operation` within the launches it has left; a record whose active operation the store does not hold as a planned `fork_provision` operation has no migration.
- `provisioning.waiting_human` without a pending action, or with the pending action `provision_fork` of the retired `/upstreaming provision`, becomes `provisioning.idle`, and the pending action is cleared. The pending actions the controller writes in that wait (`provisioning_launch_exhausted`, `provisioning_operation_failed`) are states of the table and are not touched.
- `development.idle` or `development.waiting_capacity` without a provisioned fork (a fork intent retired while destination writes were disabled, or a patch proposed before the fork) becomes `provisioning.idle` at a new revision, so the new fork intent never takes the id of the retired one.
- `development.idle` or `development.waiting_capacity` that holds a proposal never published on the provisioned fork becomes `development.idle` at a new revision, so fresh patch Work is requested.
- A proposal held without having been published is cleared, together with the publication pending for it. Nothing else changes: the fork, the dossier, the scope, the flag, the budgets, and the replay fences are kept.
- A record with requested Work, an active operation where none is expected, a publication pending for another run, a proposal held in any other state, or a state of the `investigation` or `provisioning` phase that the table does not have has no migration: the controller stops with an error and writes nothing. The state publisher's final check refuses any commit that writes one of these shapes. The migration is idempotent.

## Remote operations

Every remote effect is an operation (`.state/operations/`), whose states and transitions are the operation table, `lifecycle.OPERATIONS`. `recovery.py` is its engine and the one write path of an operation's `status`:

- `recovery.plan` takes the initial verb `record_intent`: the operation is `planned`, before any effect.
- `recovery.apply_operation(op, verb, ...)` takes one row from the operation's status: every guard is a named predicate in `recovery.PREDICATES`, either a fact the caller establishes or a check of the command (`resolve-operation` names this operation, a resolution evidence record with the id the command names, the disposition). The row's effects are applied exactly: `external_ids` merged, one `discovery` entry appended, `resolution` recorded from the command, its actor, and the evidence. A refused row changes nothing; a row the table does not have raises.
- The caller names its observation with the helpers: `mark_applying` (`begin_effect`: capacity available, the contribution not paused, the final check before the effect passed), `mark_result` (`record_response` for a success or a lost response, `record_failure` for a definite failure), `record_discovery` (`confirm_by_discovery` for the exact resource, `record_discovery` for an absent or unrelated one; only an `uncertain` operation is discovered), `fold_receipt`, and `resolve`.

Who takes which rows:

| Operation | Rows | Taken by |
|:----------|:-----|:---------|
| `branch_update`, `branch_rewrite` | `record_intent` and `begin_effect` once the publisher's final check passed (the publication check read the contribution not paused, and the head re-read right before the push matched the lease; a push reserves no runner capacity), then `record_response` or `record_failure`, then a discovery row | the publisher (`fork_publish.py`); an `uncertain` operation is discovered again by the branch observation, and an operation the store holds as `applying` (its push response was lost) first takes `record_response`. When the final check does not pass, no intent is recorded and the outcome carries no operation. |
| `fork_provision`, `dev_pr_create`, `dev_pr_update` | `record_intent` by the controller; `begin_effect`, `record_response`, and `confirm_by_discovery` when the verified receipt of the trusted workflow is folded with the exact readback (`fold_receipt`): the receipt states that the workflow's final check (the operation planned and current, the contribution not paused) passed and the call returned. A verified provisioning receipt whose status is not `provisioned` reports a definite failure: `begin_effect` and `record_failure` (`fold_failure_receipt`) | controller, reconciler |
| `debug_cleanup`, `internal_pr_closure`, `submission_comment` | `record_intent` by the controller for each attempt; `fold_receipt` for a verified receipt with its readback, `fold_failure_receipt` for a receipt that reports a failed attempt. See [The handoff operations](#the-handoff-operations). | controller, reconciler |
| `issue_body_repair` | the same three rows after the exact readback of the issue body | `canary_recovery.py` |
| every kind | `resolve_confirmed`, `resolve_failed`, `resolve_not_launched` | `/upstreaming resolve-operation` |

A planned operation that a trusted workflow runs stays `planned` across its launches: each launch is counted on the operation in `external_ids.launch_attempts`, the counter of `operation_launch`, and a launch is repeated only after `LAUNCH_INTERVAL_SECONDS` (300) without a receipt. For a record in `provisioning.idle` the launch is the lifecycle row `start_operation`: the controller records the intent and takes the row in the same tick, the row charges `operation_launch`, and the controller counts the launch on the operation. Once the budget is exhausted (`retries.infrastructure_per_head_failure` plus one launches) and the last launch is older than the interval, the controller takes `require_provisioning_intervention` to `provisioning.waiting_human` with the pending action `provisioning_launch_exhausted` and the counter `operation_launch`; nothing is launched while the contribution is paused. A resume that starts `operation_launch` again sets the launches of the planned operation back to zero. The launches of a development PR operation are counted the same way, within the same limit, without a row of the table.

`resume_provisioning` requires that the prerequisite is corrected. The controller establishes that fact for `/upstreaming resume` in `provisioning.waiting_human` when the pending action is one it wrote: `provisioning_launch_exhausted` with the counter `operation_launch`, or `provisioning_operation_failed`. The human's resume is the statement that the cause is corrected. After exhausted launches the planned operation is still open: the resume bumps the revision, starts its launches again from zero, and the same operation is launched again from `provisioning.idle`. The provisioning workflow accepts an operation of an earlier revision when the record names it as its active operation. After a failed operation the record names no operation: the resume returns to `provisioning.idle`, where a new operation is planned at the new revision, with a new id. A provisioning receipt that arrives while the record waits closes the operation and keeps the wait, so the resume also plans a new operation then.

An operation id is derived from the contribution, the revision, the kind, and the reason ids (`recovery.operation_id`), so the same intent yields the same id. An id is never used again once its operation closed: a publish-only retry of the same Result adds `publish-attempt:<attempt>` to the reason ids from the second attempt on (the attempt the retry was dispatched with), and a CI bootstrap `dev_pr_update` planned again after a failed or never-launched one adds `replan:<n>`, the number of earlier ones for the same head. The first attempt and the first plan keep the id they always had.

An operation that closes fires the lifecycle row bound to it in `recovery.FIRES`, by the state of the contribution and the end state of the operation, through `engine.apply_transition` (trigger `operation`): a confirmed `fork_provision` operation in `provisioning.idle` takes `confirm_provisioning` to `development.fix_needed` with the fork, without a revision bump; a failed one takes `require_provisioning_intervention` with the pending action `provisioning_operation_failed` and no counter. The handoff operations fire their confirm, retry, and exhaustion rows, and the cleanup `return_for_repair`. When several rows are bound, the one whose guards hold is taken: the caller supplies the count of the budget for a failed handoff attempt, and none for an invalid cleanup, so exactly one applies. `active_operation_id` names an open operation only: the record's reference is released in the same write when the operation closes, also when no row is bound (a receipt that arrives in a human wait closes the operation and keeps the state).

`/upstreaming resolve-operation operation=<id> disposition=confirmed|failed|not-launched evidence=<id>` is accepted from an authorized command actor (checked at intake) and takes the one `resolve_*` row whose guards hold; the evidence is a record of kind `resolution` in the state store. A resolution fires the bound lifecycle row only when it establishes the row's guards: a failed provisioning operation takes `require_provisioning_intervention` with the pending action `provisioning_operation_failed` and no counter; `confirm_provisioning` needs the receipt and the readback, so a provisioning operation resolved `confirmed` only closes the operation.

An open operation (`planned`, `applying`, or `uncertain`) fences its contribution exactly as the guards of the table say: `park` (also for a record in a state of `compat.LEGACY_STATES`), `refresh`, the stale resume, `restart_generation`, and the resumes guarded by quiescence are refused with the open operation named, investigation Work is not dispatched, and the publisher's final check refuses a fork write while the record names an active operation.

The state publisher's final check refuses a commit that changes an operation's status along no sequence of rows (a closed operation never changes status). Before that, `reconcile.tick` checks every operation the tick stages against the committed one with the same check (`recovery.transition_problem`). A staged operation that fails it is an unexpected error of its contribution in the step `operation_check`: the tick is computed again without the contribution, which takes `pause_on_processing_error`, and the commit goes ahead for the others. The check admits the transitions of the table only. A test scans the source: no module other than `recovery.py` writes an operation's status.
