# Transition engine
{: .no_toc }

The [lifecycle table]({{ '/state-machine/table/' | relative_url }}) defines every state and transition of a contribution. `engine.py` applies it: `engine.apply_transition(record, verb, ...)` is the write path of the lifecycle fields `phase`, `activity`, `paused`, `revision`, and `generation`. This page describes how a transition is taken, which parts of the pipeline take their transitions through the engine, and how an existing record is brought to a table state.

## Taking a transition

`apply_transition` takes a contribution record and a verb of the table. It never performs a remote effect; it returns the new record and the event to persist, or the reason the transition is refused.

1. **Resolve the verb.** The record's state is `phase.activity`. The verb is a row of that state, a rule row, or a setter or clearer of the `paused` flag. A verb that does not leave the state is an error of the caller.
2. **Apply the mask of the flag.** While `paused` is set, a controller tick is refused, a Result takes `record_late_effect` only, and of the commands only `resume`, `park`, `refresh`, `retract-pr`, `abandon`, and `resolve-operation` apply.
3. **Evaluate every guard.** Each guard text of the table is registered once in `engine.PREDICATES` under a name. A guard is one of two kinds:
   - a *record* predicate is decided by the engine from the record, the policy, the time, and the command: the exact revision, the candidate head, the pending human action, quiescence, staleness of a stop, the reminder interval, and the budgets;
   - a *fact* is something only the caller can observe, such as a CI verdict, an operation receipt, or a free worker slot. The caller asserts the fact by its name. A fact that is not asserted does not hold.

   A transition is refused unless every guard is established. A budget remains while its count is below its limit from `policy.json`; a budget whose count lives outside the record (dispatcher runs, an operation's attempts) is refused until the caller supplies the count. The rerun counts of the current head are in the record, in `budget.reruns` (`head_sha`, `flaky`, and `interrupted`); counts recorded for another head, or no counts at all, are counts of zero for the current head. Whether a failing CI job is recorded for the head is decided from the evidence `last_ci_evidence_id` names, which the caller supplies from the store (`ci_evidence.failing_job`: the first executed blocking job of the head that did not succeed); passing evidence, evidence of another head, and review evidence name none, and without one `repair_attempts_cluster` is neither charged nor exhausted. When the record names evidence the caller does not supply, every guard that reads the failing job is refused. The controller supplies it from the store, from which evidence is never removed, so a record in a state that allocates Work whose `last_ci_evidence_id` names evidence the store does not hold is an unexpected error of that contribution: the controller takes `pause_on_processing_error` (see [Errors while a tick runs](#errors-while-a-tick-runs)) instead of deferring it silently. Two facts that state the opposite of each other, such as a CI failure on the candidate head and its absence, are refused together.
4. **Apply the effects of the row.** The engine sets and clears exactly the fields the row names. The caller supplies the value of a field the engine cannot derive; a value for a field the row does not set is an error. The requested Work is kept, cleared, or replaced as the row states. Some values are always derived by the engine: Work that a row allocates for a patch or a revision carries the pending feedback ids present at that moment (`active_work.feedback_evidence_ids`), a fresh Work graph and a Result held for a publish retry carry the same ids, and a row that records a new head starts `budget.reruns` again for that head. A row may clear a part of a field: a published Result clears exactly the feedback ids its Work carried. A generation bump resets every mutable lifecycle field and keeps identity, replay fences (the 256 newest publication artifacts folded, more than the 80 the controller collects again in a tick), and the comments due on development PRs, each pinned to its PR.
5. **Charge and restart budgets.** Each budget in the row's charges is counted. A resume starts again the one counter named in `pending_human_action.counter`, and no other.
6. **Enforce the revision rule.** The revision changes exactly when the row states a bump.
7. **Emit the event.** The event kind is the verb. Its `details` record the source and target state, the budgets charged, the counter started again with its count and the acting human, and what the caller adds (for the handoff entry, the exact head and revision).

`engine.apply_command` resolves an `/upstreaming` command to the one verb whose guards hold in the record's state and takes it. A `resume` of a paused contribution clears the flag first: it takes `resume_pause`, or `resume_stale` after a long stop in provisioning or development.

## What the pipeline takes through the engine

`engine.PIPELINE_VERBS` lists the verbs that pipeline code takes through the engine; `engine.UNWIRED_VERBS` is every other verb of the table. The two sets partition the table, and a test fails when a verb is in neither or in both. A verb is unwired only while an open decision blocks it: `engine.OPEN_DECISIONS` names, for each, the entry of the [known gaps]({{ '/state-machine/known-gaps/' | relative_url }}) page that records the decision, and a test requires the two to be equal and the entry to name the verb. Today no decision is open, so every verb of the table is taken by the pipeline. `uv run --locked python -m engine` prints both sets, the open decisions, and the number of record and fact guards.

Every state of the `handoff` phase is written by the engine only. The stop flag, its commands, and the reminders are taken by the engine in every phase, for a record in a state of the table:

A CI evidence record lists the jobs of its run in job id order, whatever order the API returns them in, and observing the same run again writes nothing: an evidence record that differs from the committed one only in `collected_at` is not staged, so the committed record keeps the time it was first collected.

At the submission gate the collector reads the review comments and CI on the candidate head in one observation and then takes at most one of the two rows that leave the gate for repair. Their guards are exclusive: when CI failed on the candidate head, `return_for_repair` applies and, in the same observation, the review is recorded by `record_actionable_review` in `development.fix_needed`; the two events keep separate causes, and the repair Work requested next carries the failing CI evidence and the review; otherwise `record_actionable_review` applies. The ids of the comments a row recorded are added to the replay fences of the record in the same write.

A worker Result is folded only when it matches the Work identity: the contribution is in a working phase (`planning`, `provisioning`, `development`, or `upstream_review`), it is not paused, the generation and revision the Result's task names are the record's, and the Result answers the requested Work (its Work id, `active_work.dedupe_key`) or the trusted publication of the same worker run (a publish-only retry). In `development.publishing` the held patch pins the Result instead (`held_patch`: the worker run, the Work id, and the revision of the Work that wrote it, which its review left behind), and only a publication is folded there (published, rejected, blocked, an infrastructure error, or uncertain): a verification or a `no_change` report of the held worker run, collected again in a later artifact, takes `record_late_effect`, and a review of a held patch is folded in `development.reviewing` only. Each phase requests its own Work only (planning Work in `planning`, patch Work in `development`, revision Work in `upstream_review`), so a Result of Work of another phase is late as well; a plan is folded in `planning` only, and a Result held for a publish retry in `development` and `upstream_review` only. `park` and `pause` clear the requested Work and the pending publication without a revision bump, so an equal revision alone is not a match. Every other Result takes `record_late_effect`, in every state: a Result of another generation, of another revision, of other Work, a Result that arrives when the record holds no requested Work, a Result for a record that waits for a human, and an older publication artifact of the same Work once the newest is folded. The reconciler marks the publication artifact as processed and appends one event with the Result's status, task generation, revision, and Work id, and nothing else in the record changes. An artifact that is already marked as processed is not recorded again, so each late Result is recorded once.

A stop invalidates what is held for publication. `park_contribution`, `abandon`, the verbs that set `paused`, and every other transition that ends the generation clear `pending_publication` and `held_patch` together with the requested Work; `python -m lifecycle check` verifies it. `proposal_artifact` only ever holds a published Result, which a stop keeps: no patch is held without a write to the provisioned fork, and a worker outcome `proposal`, which no publisher produces any more, is rejected in trusted validation. Nothing is therefore published after a stop: the resume finds nothing held and requests fresh Work. The publication Result that arrives afterwards takes `record_late_effect`. No publication is launched for a paused record: the publish-only retry skips it. A stop therefore never keeps `/upstreaming resume` from applying because of a publication that was pending when it was taken.

A branch that the worker run already pushed is recorded by head observation, which reads the fork's development branch `riscv64-dev` in `development.fix_needed`, `development.waiting_ci`, and `handoff.awaiting_submission`, also while the record is paused: the observed head replaces the recorded one and the revision is bumped. The recorded head is the head of the branch the phase works on (`lifecycle.recorded_head`): `fork.head_sha` of `riscv64-dev`, and in upstream review the head of the upstream PR from `riscv64` (`upstream_pr.head_sha`, which `verify_upstream_pr` records), so the first observation after the verification is no head change and bumps no revision. Upstream revision Work names that head as its expected head; how upstream review observes it is described in [Upstream review and release](#upstream-review-and-release). While the record holds requested Work, or a Result held for a publish retry, a changed head is not recorded: the table has no head row in an `agent_running` state, so the Result is folded at the revision its Work was requested for, and the head it published is recorded by the observation after it. In `development.fix_needed`, where Work is due, a head that moved is recorded by `record_head_change`, which leads to `development.waiting_ci` (see [Development](#development)). A record that waits for a human in a state of the table that has no observation row (a state of kind `human_wait` with a recorded pending action, such as `development.waiting_human` or `upstream_review.waiting_human`) is not observed either: its head, CI, and review comments are read again once a resume, `park`, or `refresh` has left the wait, so observation never changes its state, revision, evidence, or pending action. Its branch is still read, to discover its open branch operations, and a resume from it requests no development Work in a tick whose observation read `riscv64-dev` at another head than the recorded one: Work built on the recorded head would be stale, so the next observation records the head by `record_head_change` in `development.fix_needed` first, and Work, when still due, is requested on it (`controller._unrecorded_head`). A parked or closed record is not observed. Its recorded head stays as it was, and the pushed commit stays on the fork branch; a refresh from `parked.idle` begins a new generation that records the fork and its head again at provisioning.

Every state the pipeline writes is a state of the table, and only `engine.py` decides a state change. It is the one module that assigns a field an effect of the table names (`engine.FENCED_FIELDS`: the state, the flag, the revision, the generation, the requested Work, and every field a row sets or clears, by its top-level name); a test scans the source of every other module for such assignments. The plan decides by the table's rows (see [The plan decides](#the-plan-decides)), provisioning is planned, launched, and confirmed from `provisioning.idle` (see [Remote operations](#remote-operations)), development follows the rows described in [Development](#development), and upstream review and release those in [Upstream review and release](#upstream-review-and-release).

A few fenced fields are recorded beside the row a tick takes, or without a row where the table has none; each is named in `engine.BESIDE_ROW` with the fields, or the parts of a field, it may change, and goes through `engine.record_beside`, which refuses any other field and any change of the state, the flag, the revision, or the generation:

| `operation_released` | `active_operation_id` | a closed operation is released: `active_operation_id` names an open operation only |
| `upstream_identity_bound` | `upstream.repository_id`, `upstream.base_ref`, `upstream.base_sha` | the upstream identity and base, bound once per generation from trusted API data, in the tick that restarts a generation from `parked` as well |

| `dispatcher_run_decided` | `active_work.dispatched_run_id` | beside `retry_dispatch` |

| `scope_checked` | `scope_coverage_complete` | beside `admit_development` and `readmit_development` |
| `patch_published` | `scope_paths`, `budget.no_progress_streak`, `budget.review_rejections` | beside `record_publish_success` and `record_revision_publish_success`: a published patch starts `no_progress` and `dev_reviews` again |
| `ci_rerun_recorded` | `active_operation_id` | a verified receipt of a `ci_rerun` operation the collector planned releases it (no row is fired by it) |
| `publication_answered`, `publish_retry_launched` | `pending_publication` | the Result of a publish-only retry is folded, or the retry is launched |
| `review_comment_launched`, `review_comment_answered` | `review_comments` | the post of a comment of a patch published after successive failed reviews is launched, or its receipt is folded (posted, found, failed, skipped, or lost), or the oldest exhausted comment beyond the bound is dropped with an alert |
| `review_comment_due` | `review_comments` | beside `record_late_effect`, beside the row that folds a Result, beside `record_head_change`, beside the discovery of a branch operation, or beside a command (`resolve-operation`, or one that moves the record out of a human wait): a late `published` Result of a patch published after successive failed reviews, or trusted branch evidence that its reviewed commit is on the development branch, makes the comment of that review due |

The final check before a fork write (`lifecycle.publication_check`) reads the table for a record in a handoff state: an automated writer may write the fork only in a handoff state the table marks as writing, which is `handoff.building_upstream_branch` alone. At the submission gate it may not. A publication writes the branch of its phase (`lifecycle.work_ref`): `riscv64-dev` in development, with the record naming `riscv64-dev` as its development branch, and `riscv64`, the branch of the upstream PR, in upstream review.

State validation applies the same rule to committed records: a contribution is in a table state, and a handoff wait carries a pending action with which the table enters that wait.

## Work identity and budgets

Work is allocated by the rows of the table, and every allocation is an event whose kind is the verb. The controller considers a contribution in `planning.idle` (or `queued.idle`, which first takes `begin_planning`), `development.fix_needed`, `handoff.review_due`, `handoff.review_pending`, or `upstream_review.fix_needed` that is not paused and holds no Work, pending publication, or open operation. When the guard of an exhaustion row of that state holds, the row is taken in place of the allocation, without a worker slot. Otherwise the allocation row whose guards hold is taken once a worker slot (and, for write Work, capacity) is free; while none is free the contribution is reported as deferred and nothing changes. Every row that allocates Work, and `allocate_work_retry`, is guarded by `upstream identity and base bound` (`upstream.repository_id`, `upstream.base_ref`, `upstream.base_sha`), since the Work payload names them; while they are not bound the contribution is reported as deferred with that guard. The controller binds them from the verified upstream snapshot before commands are applied, for every record that is not parked, and again once the commands are applied, for a record that left `parked` in this tick: `restart_generation` clears them, except the target branch the request asked for at intake, which it keeps as `upstream.base_ref` (read from the issue description frozen at intake; a record created before intake froze it keeps none), and the collector reads the upstream of a parked contribution when its tracking issue carries a refresh command the controller can still take: a comment that parses as `/upstreaming refresh`, by an actor of `command_authority`, that the command ledger has not consumed. It reads the upstream at that target branch, or at its default branch when the request named none. A snapshot of another branch than the one the record names binds nothing, and the contribution is reported as deferred. A refresh from `parked` therefore allocates its planning Work in the same tick when the snapshot is available and the record is not paused (a restart accepted while paused binds the identity and allocates nothing until a resume). When the upstream base branch cannot be read, the record is handled as [The upstream base branch](#the-upstream-base-branch) states: it waits for a human at once when the upstream answered that the branch does not exist or was renamed, and otherwise stays in `queued.idle` within `source_reads`, a later tick binding it. `/upstreaming refresh branch=NAME` from `parked` restarts on the branch `NAME` instead of the requested one; a later restart without `branch` returns to the requested one.

The Work identity is the generation, the revision, and the Work id. The Work id is `active_work.dedupe_key`, `<contribution>@g<generation>.r<revision>:<task>`, and the queue graph is derived from it, so Work of another generation or revision is another graph and a terminal graph is never submitted again. Once `request_ci_rerun` has charged `flaky_reruns` for the head, the id also names that count (`<contribution>@g<generation>.r<revision>.rerun<count>:<task>`): the row keeps the revision, so the repair Work requested after a rerun is not the Work whose Result requested it. A row that keeps the revision can allocate Work whose graph returned a Result in the same tick or is completed or cancelled in the queue: after a revision Result that left the revision unchanged, failing CI is answered at the same revision. That Work is terminal without a usable Result and is never submitted: in the same tick the controller takes `allocate_work_retry`, a fresh graph at a new revision, within `work_recovery` and `work_total`, or `exhaust_work_recovery` to the human wait of the phase. `active_work.generation` records the generation. The Work payload (`task.json`) carries `generation` and `dedupe_key` beside `revision`, and the reconciler and the publisher's final check (`lifecycle.publication_check`) compare all three with the record: the publisher writes the fork only for the record's generation and revision, and only while the record holds that Work, or a Result held for a publish retry. A payload written before the identity carried the generation names neither `generation` nor `dedupe_key`; it is generation 0, its Work is recognized by its revision, and its Result can never be folded into a later generation. Requested Work recorded without `active_work.generation` is generation 0 in the same way, and a replay of it submits the payload it was first submitted with, without the new fields.

The task the Work asks a worker for follows the row: `dispatch_planning` asks for a planning run, `request_repair` for a repair of the failing CI job, `request_patch` for a patch, or for a repair when feedback is pending for it to answer, and the two upstream rows for a revision. Work that a row allocates carries the pending feedback ids present at allocation (`active_work.feedback_evidence_ids`), and the payload passes exactly those ids to the worker in `feedback_evidence_ids`; feedback recorded later is not carried. Its reason ids are the carried feedback, then the CI evidence and the plan. Every Work also carries its history: the reports of the successful workers before it, what followed them, and the owner's steering (see [Worker reports, history and steering](#worker-reports-history-and-steering)).

The budgets of `BUDGETS` are counted where the table charges them:

A rejected or blocked outcome charges nothing else: `work_total` and the cluster are charged when the next Work is allocated. A resume from a human wait an exhausted budget caused starts again exactly the counter the pending action names.

### The upstream base branch

The collector reads the upstream repository and the base branch the record names (`upstream.base_ref`, or the default branch while none is named) for every contribution that is not parked or terminal, and the controller binds or verifies the identity from it. It reads one branch per upstream; every failed read carries the branch it asked for (`ref`), and the controller never applies the answer for one branch to a record that names another: such a snapshot is reported as deferred and charges nothing. An intake request for an upstream that already has a contribution that is not terminal reads nothing. GitHub answers a read of a renamed branch with a redirect to the new name; a response that names another branch is not that branch. Where the base branch is needed, in `queued.idle`, `planning.idle`, `provisioning.idle`, `development.fix_needed`, `handoff.review_due`, `handoff.review_pending`, and `upstream_review.fix_needed` (`lifecycle.BASE_BRANCH_WAITS`), a read that fails is decided in the tick, after the commands:

- When the upstream answers definitively that the branch does not exist (HTTP 404 for the branch of a repository that was read) or was renamed (the redirect), the controller takes `require_base_branch` to the human wait of the phase (`planning.waiting_human` for a request in `queued.idle`, whose phase has none) with `pending_human_action` `base_branch_unavailable` and no counter.
- When GitHub answers the read of the repository for the recorded repository id (`upstream.repository_id`, or any id while none is bound) under another name, the repository was renamed or transferred: a definitive answer, so the controller takes `require_renamed_upstream` at once (`pending_human_action` `upstream_unavailable`, no counter), without spending the retry window on a read that cannot change; no failed read is counted for it.
- Any other failure (a network or server error, a rate limit, a repository that cannot be read, HTTP 404 or 403 for the repository included) takes `record_source_unavailable`, which keeps the state and charges `source_reads`. The time since the first failed read of the run (`budget.source_unavailable_since`) is cut into spacing slots of `retries.source_unavailable_minutes` divided by `retries.source_unavailable_reads` (`engine.source_read_slot_length`, 20 minutes), and a failed read is counted only when it is the first of a run or falls in a later slot than the last counted read (`budget.source_unavailable_at`); a failed read in the same slot writes nothing and is reported as deferred (one deferred entry per contribution and tick), so a burst of ticks adds no events and no commits. Ticks do not come on time: a scheduled run of `controller.yml` is delayed or skipped, and most ticks come from issue, comment, workflow run, and push events. Because the slots are counted from the first failed read rather than from the last counted one, a regular tick counts once per slot whatever its jitter (a minute early or late, or every 3 minutes), and the wait begins at the first tick once the minimum time has passed. After a long pause between ticks, the next failed read counts once, like any other. Its count, `budget.source_unavailable`, counts the counted failed reads, `budget.source_unavailable_since` records when the first of them failed, and `budget.source_unavailable_repository` counts those that could not read the repository itself; a tick that reads the upstream clears them all (the write `upstream_read` beside the rows), and a resume of the wait starts them again. The budget is exhausted by the first failed read once `retries.source_unavailable_minutes` (60) minutes have passed since the first failed read and the counted failed reads, that read included when it is counted, reach `retries.source_unavailable_reads` (3): `controller.yml` also ticks on issues, comments, workflow runs, and pushes, so a burst of ticks alone never exhausts it, and a schedule that skips runs for hours still leads to the wait at the third spaced read instead of days later. The read that meets both conditions is never held back by the spacing (`engine.source_reads_spent`); it takes `require_upstream_access` (`upstream_unavailable`) when most counted failed reads, this one included when it is counted, could not read the repository itself, and `exhaust_source_reads` (`pending_human_action` `base_branch_unavailable`) otherwise; both name `source_reads` as the counter, and the event records both counts. Which rows are the exhaustion of a budget is declared on the row (`Transition.exhausts`), never read from the wording of its guards.

The tracking-issue entry states which cause applied in plain words: the branch does not exist, it was renamed and to which name, reads of it kept failing (how many were counted, since when, how many could not read the repository itself, and the last error), the repository could not be read, or GitHub now names it otherwise. It then says what can be done: a wait that failed reads caused continues on its own once the upstream can be read again, and a resume reads it again at once; a repository GitHub names otherwise continues on its own if GitHub answers for the same repository under its recorded name again (or, for a request whose id GitHub did not give, says that only a resume continues), and a resume before then only waits again; for a missing or renamed base branch, resume once it can be read again, or, to continue on another branch, park first and then, once it is parked, post `/upstreaming refresh revision=N branch=NAME reason="..."` (named with the new name of a rename); while the upstream PR is open, no restart is offered, since `restart_generation` refuses it. For the repository, a repository that moved is a new request (park, then abandon). The plain words name no state either.

A wait that failed reads caused (`pending_human_action.counter` is `source_reads`: `exhaust_source_reads` and `require_upstream_access`) heals itself: the collector keeps reading its upstream on every tick, and once a tick reads the repository and the recorded base branch again, with the recorded repository id, the controller takes `resume_upstream_read` after the commands (so that a resume posted in the same tick is taken as posted). It is the same transition as the posted resume of the wait (`lifecycle.UPSTREAM_READ_RESUMES`): a new revision, the pending action cleared, and `source_reads` started again; the tracking issue states that the contribution continues (`status_ledger.NOTICE_VERBS`). A paused wait takes neither: once a resume clears the stop (`resume_pause`), a tick that reads the upstream again ends it. The wait a renamed repository caused (`require_renamed_upstream`: `upstream_unavailable` with no counter) is read on every tick too, and once GitHub answers for the recorded repository id under its recorded name again, the controller takes `resume_renamed_upstream` the same way (it carries no counter, so it starts none); while GitHub still names it otherwise, a posted resume returns to the step (in `provisioning.waiting_human` too, where it is one of the waits the controller entered, `controller.PROVISIONING_WAITS`) and the next read waits again at once. `require_renamed_upstream` sets `upstream.repository_id` to the id GitHub answered for: the recorded one when it is bound, and for a request not bound yet the id of the renamed repository, so that the wait ends only on the same repository and another repository later created under the recorded name pauses it (`pause_on_identity_change`) instead of being bound in its place. When GitHub gave no id, nothing under the recorded name is bound while the wait lasts (the tick reports it as deferred), and only a resume continues. Once GitHub answers for the same repository id under its recorded name but the upstream says that the base branch does not exist or was renamed (the collector records the `repository_id` it read with such an answer), the controller takes `require_base_branch` in the wait itself, a tick row that keeps the state: the pending action becomes `base_branch_unavailable` with the entry it posts from the state the step was due in, and the wait is no longer read until a resume is posted. A wait the upstream's own answer about the branch caused (a missing or renamed branch, no counter) still needs a resume.

In upstream review, a base branch renamed while the upstream PR is open heals without a human, once the contribution is not paused and no operation is open (a paused contribution follows it after its resume). GitHub retargets the open PR to the new name, so when the redirect names the new branch of a contribution in upstream review, the collector also reads the PR the record names: when it is open, targets the new name of the same repository, and the new branch's history contains `upstream.base_sha`, the snapshot carries that verified base, and the controller takes `follow_renamed_base` in `upstream_review.waiting_ci` and `upstream_review.fix_needed` (the state is kept) or `resume_renamed_base` from `upstream_review.waiting_human` when the rename had caused the wait (to `upstream_review.fix_needed`, at a new revision, the pending action cleared and `source_reads` started again). Both set `upstream.base_ref` and `upstream_pr.base_ref` to the new name and keep `upstream.base_sha`, which the renamed branch still contains, and both post an entry on the tracking issue that states the rename (`status_ledger.NOTICE_VERBS`). A failed read of the PR is a failed read of the upstream, charged to `source_reads`, never a definitive answer. A PR that does not follow the rename leaves the definitive answer as it is.

Before the upstream PR is verified, in the locked `handoff.waiting_upstream_pr`, a base branch the upstream renamed after the package was posted heals the same way: a human opens the PR against the new name, and when no candidate satisfies every filter, a single candidate that satisfies every filter but the base, targets the name GitHub now redirects `upstream.base_ref` to, and whose new branch still contains `upstream.base_sha` (`collect.base_redirect` and `collect.base_contained`; only the candidates on that name count, so a candidate on another branch never makes it ambiguous), takes `verify_upstream_pr`, which then sets `upstream.base_ref` to the new name and records the rename in the event; the tracking issue states it in plain words.

While a contribution waits with `base_branch_unavailable` or `upstream_unavailable`, the collector does not read its upstream, unless failed reads caused the wait (above), a renamed repository caused it (`upstream_unavailable` with no counter, above), its tracking issue carries a resume the controller can still take (parsed, authorized, not consumed), or it waits in upstream review for a renamed base branch (so that the rename it follows ends the wait). The resume of the wait (`resume_planning`, `resume_provisioning`, `resume_development`, `resume_submission_review`, `resume_upstream_revision`) is accepted as usual, and starts `source_reads` again when it caused the wait; when the upstream still cannot be read in that tick, the wait is entered again with a new entry. A paused contribution, or one with an open operation, takes none of these rows and is reported as deferred.

## Development

Development Work is requested only in `development.fix_needed` (`request_patch`, `request_repair`, or an exhaustion row), and its Result is folded only in `development.agent_running`, by exactly one row. A development patch is reviewed before it is published: its Work carries the review contract, its worker run verifies the patch and publishes nothing, and the verified patch is held (see [The review of a development patch](#the-review-of-a-development-patch)):

| Result | Row | Leads to |
|:-------|:----|:---------|
| Verified (the series rebuilds on the expected head and passes the trusted validation; nothing was pushed) | `hold_patch_for_review` | `development.review_pending` at a new revision, with the held patch recorded in `held_patch`. |
| Published, by Work dispatched before development patches were reviewed (its payload carries no review contract) | `record_publish_success` | `development.waiting_ci`. The row records the published Result (`proposal_artifact`, with the outcome `published`, and `publication_operation_id`, the confirmed branch operation, which the development PR request requires), clears exactly the feedback ids its Work carried, and starts the rerun counts again. Beside the row the published files join `scope_paths` and `no_progress` starts again. The development PR is then requested by the row `launch_development_pr` in `development.waiting_ci` (see [Remote operations](#remote-operations)). |
| `no_change` (the development worker reports a failure not caused by the patch), the Work carries no feedback, `flaky_reruns` remains, and the outcome carries the receipt of the trusted rerun | `request_ci_rerun` | `development.waiting_ci` at the same revision, charging `flaky_reruns` for the head; the recorded CI evidence is cleared, so CI on the head is read again there. The event names the receipt (its status, the runs, the attempts read back) and the confirmed `ci_rerun` operation is recorded. A `no_change` outcome without the receipt, where the row would apply, is a failed publication (below). See [CI reruns](#ci-reruns). |
| Rejected in trusted validation, blocked, a `proposal`, or `no_change` when the Work carries feedback or `flaky_reruns` is exhausted | `reject_patch_proposal` | `development.fix_needed` at a new revision, charging `no_progress`; the pending feedback is kept. A blocked Result's rehearsal evidence is stored and named in the event. |
| Rejected as stale (the fork head moved) | `replan_development` | `development.fix_needed` at a new revision, charging nothing. |
| Uncertain (the push was not read back) | `schedule_publish_retry`, or `exhaust_publish_recovery` once `publish_recovery` is spent | The Result stays held in `development.agent_running`, as for an infrastructure error below, and its Work is cleared, so no Work recovery takes its completed graph. The branch observation discovers the operation of the push. `record_publish_success` is taken once the publication is confirmed: when the publish-only retry reads back the head the lost push left (it confirms the open operation of the earlier attempt when its Result is folded) or publishes the same commit, or when its Result finds the operation of the earlier attempt confirmed, including a retry rejected as stale behind a confirmed operation (the head moved after the push landed; the later head is recorded by `record_head_change`). The row then names the confirmed operation in `publication_operation_id`. |
| An infrastructure error of the publication (also a rerun that was refused or not read back, and a `no_change` outcome without a rerun receipt where `request_ci_rerun` applies), `publish_recovery` remains | `schedule_publish_retry` | `development.agent_running` with the Result held in `pending_publication` (carrying the feedback ids of its Work) and the Work cleared; `publish_recovery` is charged. |
| An infrastructure error, `publish_recovery` exhausted | `exhaust_publish_recovery` | `development.waiting_human` with the pending action `publication_infrastructure_failure` that names `publish_recovery`; the held publication is marked exhausted. |

Every development Result carries the worker's report (`report.json`). It is stored as `worker_report` evidence when `hold_patch_for_review`, `record_publish_success` or `request_ci_rerun` folds the Result, or when the Result's push may have landed (an `uncertain` Result held for a retry), and discarded by every other row. A report stored at the hold records the commit the patch rebuilds to as `head_candidate`; the publication of the reviewed patch stores the same evidence id again with that commit as `head_after`. When CI then fails on the published head, the repair Work receives that report beside the CI failure, in its history.

A publish-only retry is launched once `pending_publication.next_attempt_at` is due, for a record in `development.agent_running` or `development.publishing` that is not stopped: 15 minutes after the first failed publication, 60 after the second, and 240 after every later one. The launch records `pending_publication.launched_at` and charges nothing. The retry workflow uploads its Result as the `porting-publication` artifact with the task of the worker run it publishes and that run's id, and the controller collects it like the Result of a worker run, from completed `workflow_dispatch` runs on `main`, whoever dispatched them; the same rows fold it. Only a retry names the worker run it publishes (`worker-run-id`): the collector deletes such a file from a planner or worker artifact, whose Result is the Result of its own run. The Result of a retry ends the held publication: `pending_publication` is cleared beside the row that folds it. A launched retry whose Result is not folded within 60 minutes is a failed publication: `schedule_publish_retry` charges `publish_recovery` and schedules the next retry after the backoff, or `exhaust_publish_recovery` waits for a human once the budget is spent, so a retry that never returns a Result ends in the human wait. Each failed publication is charged once: an infrastructure error that the Result of an already charged launch reports when it arrives later (its attempt is not above `pending_publication.attempts`, or no retry is launched) takes `record_late_effect` and charges nothing again. A held publication that is exhausted launches nothing more and is not pending: `resume_development` applies to its wait, clears it, and requests fresh Work, on the head the observation records once a push of the exhausted publication landed.

`request_ci_rerun` is taken only once the failed jobs were rerun on the exact head and the new attempt was read back, or nothing needed rerunning (see [CI reruns](#ci-reruns)). `development.waiting_ci` after it records no pending action and no reminder, like the wait after a publish: CI itself ends it. The row is shown in the event log of the status page (the tracking issue receives no entry, since `development.waiting_ci` is not a human wait), and `/upstreaming refresh` and `/upstreaming park` leave the wait.

A fork head that moved while development Work is due (a push to `riscv64-dev` in `development.fix_needed`) takes `record_head_change` to `development.waiting_ci` at a new revision: no Work is requested for the head the record named, the CI evidence of that head is cleared, pending feedback is kept and is answered after CI passes on the new head, and the new head is observed in the same tick as below. On an unchanged head, `development.fix_needed` reads the review feedback of the development PR only: an actionable review takes `record_actionable_review` there and is carried by the Work requested next; CI, explanation-only comments, and the PR match are not read. In `development.waiting_ci`, in order: a new fork head takes `record_head_change`; a development PR that does not match takes `record_pr_mismatch`; an explanation-only comment takes `record_explanation`; an actionable review takes `record_actionable_review`, which ends the observation (a CI failure observed with it is not recorded, since `development.fix_needed` reads no CI, and the review would no longer match the head once the repair is published); then CI on the exact head, read with jobs that lost their runner classified, is not acted on while it is behind a confirmed rerun, and otherwise takes `record_ci_failure` for an executed blocking failure, `rerun_interrupted_ci` or `exhaust_interrupted_reruns` for a required job that was cancelled or lost its runner (see [CI reruns](#ci-reruns)), `nominate_for_handoff` when every required job passed, no actionable feedback is pending, and the series meets the series requirements of the verified plan, `return_for_feedback` when feedback is pending, or `return_for_series_requirements` when no feedback is pending and a requirement is unmet. The series requirements (a plan of schema version 4 records them; an earlier plan records none and needs no read) are read once when CI passes without pending feedback: one comparison of the upstream base and the head in the fork, and, when the series holds `{debug}` commits, one read of each other commit, so a path that only a `{debug}` commit changes, or that a later commit reverts, does not count. A series of more than 50 commits or 300 files or more is not checked and is nominated with a note in the event; a failed read skips the contribution until the next tick, as every failed read of the observation does. The Work requested after `return_for_series_requirements` carries the unmet requirements as `context.series_requirements`, only while the fork head is the head the evidence names, and the trusted worker gate rejects a `patch` of that Work whose non-debug commits do not change a matching path, which `reject_patch_proposal` charges to `no_progress`; a worker that finds a requirement does not apply reports `blocked`. Repeated rejection reaches the automatic refresh, where the planner can correct the requirement, then `exhaust_repair_attempts` or `exhaust_work_total`. Passing CI behind an open operation keeps the contribution in `development.waiting_ci`, and so does a run that waits for approval: that is collector health, not a contribution state. A planned or launched development PR operation keeps nomination and Work back until its receipt closes it, its launches are exhausted, or it is closed because it no longer matches the record. A repair that CI on its head no longer needs is not withdrawn: its Result is folded by the rows above, or arrives late.

Requested Work is recovered by the rows of `development.agent_running`: a dispatcher run that ended with the Work absent from the queue or unclaimed takes `retry_dispatch` (the dispatcher is launched again for the same committed request) within `dispatch_recovery`, and `exhaust_dispatch_recovery` once it is spent; each ended run is decided once, and the record names the last one in `active_work.dispatched_run_id`. A worker run that ends without a Result (cancelled, stopped by its 120 minute timeout, or lost with its runner) is not a lost dispatch: gh-aw cancels its Claim and holds the Work for another attempt after its backoff, within the `retry` attempts of the pool's Policy (3), and cancels the Work itself once they are spent. The controller reads that retry from the validating replay (`queue_result.work_retries`: available Work with `attempts` and `retry_not_before`), counts the dispatcher runs only from the retry time, and when none was created within 15 minutes of it (`DISPATCH_LAUNCH_GRACE_MINUTES`, the grace of every dispatcher launch, so a run list that lags behind a dispatcher run created since launches no second one), launches the dispatcher without a row and without charging anything (the entry of the Work in the tick report names `queue_retry`); a Work whose `retry_not_before` is no representable time is left out of the retries and reported in the controller log, and the others are read as usual; a dispatcher run after the retry time that leaves the Work unclaimed is charged as usual, and its event names the retry (`queue_retry`, and `why` in plain words, which the tracking issue entry of the human wait shows). A Work the queue cancelled after its last attempt takes `allocate_work_retry` or `exhaust_work_recovery`. The dispatcher never submits again Work the queue already holds: a Work is immutable in the queue, and the payload the dispatcher builds for it changes with the control commit it pins and with a history recorded after the submission, so a second submission would be refused (`work_conflict`, which the trusted control step can only report as an unclassified failure that requires recovery). Its staging step (`scripts/work_queue_stage.cjs`) reads the ledger through the gh-aw library of the run and stages only the Work the queue does not hold, followed by the dispatch request, or the dispatch request alone when every planned Work is queued; a queued Work is dispatched with the payload it was first submitted with, and a payload built differently is reported as payload drift in the step log. When the ledger cannot be read, the plan is staged unchanged. The dispatcher agent that runs after the step can stage intents of its own through gh-aw's work-queue tools, and gh-aw rejects extra intents only in a batch that holds the plan's intents, so the staged intents are the step's output. Before the agent runs, the staging script is copied from the run's checkout into `${RUNNER_TEMP}/gh-aw/trusted-intent-check`, which the agent's sandbox mounts read-only, and the staging step loads it from there. The dispatcher agent has no shell and cannot write files (`tools.bash: false`, `tools.edit: false`). gh-aw passes these to Pi as two `--exclude-tools` flags and Pi 1.5.0 keeps only the last one, so the workflow repeats `--exclude-tools bash,edit,write` last in `engine.args` and sets the sub-agents' arguments (`GH_AW_PI_SUBAGENT_ARGS`) with the same value. Its tools are Pi's `read`, its MCP tools, and Pi's `codemode` and `tool_search`, which only call or find the agent's other tools (a `codemode` script runs in a QuickJS sandbox with no file system or network). Before the agent, the directories of the runner's home that AWF mounts into the sandbox read-write (among them `~/.local/bin` and `~/.local/lib`, where the rootless AWF is installed, and `~/.cargo`) are made owned by root, so nothing the sandbox's user can change is run on the runner after the agent, by awf at its teardown or in a later step that looks a command up on PATH. A listed directory that is a link fails the step, and the final check of ownership and modes follows no link. AWF's entrypoint makes `~/.claude` writable to every user again (mode 777, its `settings.json` 666) because the API proxy holds `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY`; that is benign, since `~/.claude` is not on PATH and nothing on the runner runs from it. The step covers only these home directories: the sandbox still mounts `/tmp/gh-aw` and the workspace, with its `.git`, read-write. After the agent, one step loads the check from that copy and writes back exactly the staged intents (none when no plan was staged) and no finish intent, since a dispatcher holds no Claim, drops every other intent and names it in a warning. It runs no command from PATH, follows no link and restores the write permission the agent could have removed (`chmod 555` on `/tmp/gh-aw`); a collected file it cannot unlink is emptied in place, and any other final state fails the step. When the check cannot start or fails, the step links a new name under `/tmp/gh-aw` to `/`, which makes gh-aw's final recovery check fail (it scans all of `/tmp/gh-aw`), and that check gates both agent artifact uploads, so no unchecked intent is uploaded. The name is random (`work-queue.unchecked-` and 32 hex digits), so the agent cannot take it first; the link is read back, another random name is tried when it fails, and when no link can be planted the step's failure says that the run's agent artifacts may hold unchecked intents. No step of the workflow after the agent checks out code, runs git or loads code from the workspace, which the sandbox mounts read-write. gh-aw's own steps run git there: "Configure Git credentials" runs `git rev-parse`, `git config` and `git remote`, and the post step of the checkout runs `git config`, so both read the workspace's `.git`, which the dispatcher agent has no tool to write. A Work graph that the queue reports cancelled takes `allocate_work_retry` or `exhaust_work_recovery` at once: the queue cancels Work only after its last Claim ended without a Result, and the publish job of a worker runs only for a completed outcome. A graph the queue reports completed becomes completed in the `safe_outputs` job of the worker, before its `publish` job uploads the `porting-publication` artifact, so it takes these rows only once its Result is known to be missing. A publication collected in the tick that names the Work and left it requested (a late or already processed Result) decides it at once. Otherwise the reconciler reads the worker run the queue bound to the graph (`queue_result.work_runs`: the run of the dispatch of the Work's Claim, else the run named by its Result's artifact output) with `collect.worker_run_result` (the run, and the artifacts of an ended run): a run that ended, or that GitHub no longer has, without an unexpired `porting-publication` artifact, and a graph the queue binds to no run, decide it at once; an artifact on an ended run that no tick collected (its download failed, the trusted recovery rejected it, or the run is outside the inventory of 20 runs) is waited for 60 minutes after the run ended (`RESULT_COLLECTION_GRACE_MINUTES`, the wait of a launched publish-only retry), then decides it. While the run has not ended, or could not be read in the tick, nothing is decided and nothing is charged: the entry of the Work in the tick report says `waiting for worker run <id> to publish its Result` or `waiting: the worker run of the completed Work was not read`, no dispatcher is launched and the record keeps its state and Work. The event of the row names the reason in its details: `worker_run`, `run_conclusion`, and `result` (`missing`, `uncollected`, `unbound`, `collected`, or `cancelled`). A worker run that never ends keeps the contribution waiting, bounded by the job timeouts of GitHub, and a Result collected after the wait retried the Work takes `record_late_effect`. Nothing reads `active_work.deadline_at`. The submission rewrite and review Work, in `handoff.agent_running` and `handoff.reviewing`, are recovered by the same rows of their states. The relaunch a dispatch recovery decides stands in a tick that also observes CI, unless the record no longer holds its Work once observed.

gh-aw refuses every dispatch (`work_queue_policy_proposal_mismatch`) while the work-queue Policy installed in the ledger declares a worker profile the compiled dispatcher policy no longer declares, for example after a worker workflow is renamed; the ref and logical contract of a profile change with every compilation and never cause it. The controller reads the compiled policy from the dispatcher lock (`queue_policy.py`) and compares it with the validating replay of the ledger. While the installed Policy drops a compiled profile and the queue is not quiescent, a dispatch is not decided: nothing is charged and nothing is launched. On a quiescent queue the dispatcher's first steps publish the compiled policy as a new Policy epoch, an administrator `policy` request authenticated as the dispatcher run (`scripts/work_queue_policy.cjs`), before any Work is staged; the controller launches the dispatcher for it at most once every 30 minutes while a record holds or waits for dispatched Work. A dispatch decided before the reinstall is charged as usual, and a record that waits with `dispatch_failed` takes `rearm_dispatch` once the installed Policy epoch was published after the failure: the same effects as the human resume of the wait, starting `dispatch_recovery` again.

## The review of a development patch

The Work payload of a development patch or repair carries the review contract (`review: required`, `fork_publish.review_required`). Its trusted publication job runs the preflight, which answers `verify` for such Work while it is the Work of `development.agent_running`, and then `python -m fork_publish verify` without any token: the same rebuild in a fresh repository, scope, `publisher.validate_update`, changed files and digest checks as a push, ending before the push. Its outcome `verified` names the digest of the series, the commit it rebuilds to (the App identity and the dates of the series make it deterministic), its tree, its changed files, and its operation. An infrastructure failure of the verification is held like a failed publication (`schedule_publish_retry`), and the publish-only retry verifies again.

`hold_patch_for_review` records the pin in `held_patch` (the worker run, the `porting-publication` artifact that carries the verified series and the run that owns it (`artifact_run_id`: the worker run, or the publish-only retry that verified the patch again after an infrastructure error, whose artifact also uploads `patches.mbox`), the Work id, its generation and revision, the digest, the commit, the tree, the expected head, the operation, the changed files, the reasons and the feedback ids of the Work, the report, the time of the hold, and `expires_at`, when the artifacts its publication reads expire at the earliest: the expiry GitHub reported for the artifact that carries the series, which the controller collects with it (`artifact-expires-at`), else the artifact retention after the Work was requested, and, when a publish-only retry owns that artifact, the retention after the Work was requested for the worker run's own artifact) and bumps the revision. In `development.review_pending` the controller allocates the review Work (`request_patch_review`, task `development_review`, priority 0, the `reviewer` profile) when a worker slot is free, `exhaust_work_total` once `work_total` is spent, and `expire_held_patch` (to `development.fix_needed`) once `held_patch.expires_at` is less than a day away. The review Work carries the reasons of the worker's Work as its reasons and no feedback; its payload names the held patch (`context.patch`) and the worker's report at the pinned control commit with its SHA-256 (`context.report`). The trusted pre-agent steps of `reviewer.md` download the series from the artifact with the job's read token (`scripts/held_patch.py`: an archive larger than 64 MiB is refused from its metadata and as soon as its download passes that bound, never buffered whole, and `patches.mbox` and `proposal.json` are inflated only within the bounds `fork_publish` and the proposal check admit, 4 MiB and 256 KiB) and check the run that owns it and its digest, rebuild it on the head it was verified on (`context.patch.expected_head`) and check its tree, and fetch the report, the plan, and the history; the model reads and diffs `./fork` (branches `base`, `head`, `patch`) and runs no reproduction. The publish job checks the Result without a token (`patch_review check`): the exact Work, the pin, findings only on changed files, every text bounded and redacted. The verdict is derived from the findings: a blocker or a major finding fails the review whatever it declares; a declared fail without one is not a usable review.

In `development.reviewing`:

| Result | Row | Leads to |
|:-------|:----|:---------|
| The review passes | `accept_patch_review` | `development.publishing`, with the held patch pending publication at once (`pending_publication` with no attempt yet, the feedback ids of the worker's Work, and the review); a passing review ends the succession of failed reviews, so `dev_reviews` starts again. |
| The review fails, `dev_reviews` remains | `reject_patch_review` | `development.fix_needed` at a new revision, charging `dev_reviews`; the review is added to the pending feedback, so the next Work is a repair that carries it, and the held patch is discarded. That Work's payload names the rejected series (`context.previous_patch`: the review evidence, the artifact and the run that owns it, the digest, the commit, the head and the operation, from the pin the review recorded), unless the artifact expired or expires within a day when the Work was requested; the worker's trusted pre-agent step downloads it with `scripts/held_patch.py` (an expired artifact is skipped, a wrong run or digest fails the step) and rebuilds it as the branch `previous-patch` when it still applies, so the worker amends that patch. |
| The review fails, `dev_reviews` exhausted | `publish_after_failed_reviews` | `development.publishing`, as for a pass; the pending publication records that the patch is published after a failed review. The review is not made pending feedback. |
| No usable review | `allocate_work_retry`, `exhaust_work_recovery` | A fresh review Work graph within `work_recovery`; its exhaustion, like that of `dispatch_recovery`, waits in `development.waiting_human` with the pending action `patch_review_unavailable` and keeps the held patch: the entry on the tracking issue says in plain words that the review could not run, and `/upstreaming resume` takes `resume_patch_review` back to `development.review_pending` for the review of the same patch (restarting `dispatch_recovery`, `work_recovery` and `work_total`). There the controller discards a held patch whose artifact expired, or that a stop during the wait discarded, by `expire_held_patch`, and fresh development Work is due. |

Every usable review is stored as `patch_review` evidence (`patch-review-<contribution>-r<revision>-<reviewer run>`): its verdict, what became of the patch (`publish`, `return_for_repair`, `publish_anyway`), the number of failed reviews in succession, the summary and findings, the answers to the findings of earlier reviews, the pin, and the report it reviewed. The history of every later Work and review carries it, linked to that report. In `development.publishing` the publish-only retry is launched in the same tick, unless `held_patch.expires_at` is less than a day away: then `expire_held_patch` discards the held patch and the pending publication, nothing is launched, and fresh development Work is due; its preflight mints the fork token, and `fork_publish publish` refuses, before any push, a series whose digest is not the held digest, and a rebuild to any other commit than the held commit; `lifecycle.publication_check` refuses a Work with the review contract outside `development.publishing`, and there, for every write whatever its payload says, requires the held patch and matches its revision and Work id instead of the record's; `fork_publish publish` enforces the pin (digest, head, commit) for every publication from that state too. Its Result takes `record_publish_success` (clearing the held patch and exactly the carried feedback; beside the row `no_progress` and `dev_reviews` start again), `schedule_publish_retry` or `exhaust_publish_recovery`, `replan_development` when the fork head moved, or `reject_patch_proposal` when the series no longer rebuilds to the reviewed commit. A rejection is taken as the publication's only from a publish-only retry that read the record in `development.publishing` (its artifact carries `publication-stage` `publish`, which the controller keeps for a retry only); a rejected verification of the same Work collected late, by the worker's own run or a retry made before the review, is a late Result (`record_late_effect`). `dev_reviews` counts failed reviews in succession only: every row that discards a patch to be published anyway unpublished (`replan_development`, `reject_patch_proposal`, a stop, and the resume or refresh after `exhaust_publish_recovery`) starts it again too, by the qualified clear `overridden budget.review_rejections`, which clears the count only when the pending publication it discards records `published_anyway`. When the published patch was published after failed reviews, `record_publish_success` adds the comment of the latest review to the comments due on the record (`review_comments`: for each, the review, the published head, the development PR it is posted on, the attempts, the next attempt, the last reason, and whether its attempts are spent); every comment still due is kept, whatever patch is published next, and a new generation keeps them too, each pinned to the PR it is posted on. The comment is due whenever a `published` Result is the publication of a patch published anyway, on time or late and in any state: the `patch_review` evidence with the disposition `publish_anyway` whose pin names the Result's worker run, Work, and commit (`patch_review.published_anyway`) makes its comment due in the same write, once per review. So a stop, a pause, or a processing error folded before that Result, which discards the held patch and the pending publication, and the late Result of a publish-only retry lost until `exhaust_publish_recovery`, still leave the comment due: the Result takes `record_late_effect`, and `review_comment_due` beside it adds the comment, named in the event (`review_comments_due`). When no `published` Result of that publication ever reaches the reconciler (its push was not read back and the Result is `uncertain`, or its run died before its upload, and a pause, a processing error, or the exhaustion of `publish_recovery` came first), trusted branch evidence makes the comment due instead, in the same write, in any state, once per review (`patch_review.landed_anyway`): a `branch_update` or `branch_rewrite` operation of the contribution that becomes `confirmed` (discovered by the branch observation, which reads the branch in a human wait and while paused too, read back by a publish-only retry, or confirmed in any Result the reconciler folds or records late, or confirmed by a human with `resolve-operation`) whose commit is the commit a `publish_anyway` review pinned (`patch.candidate`), a fork head at that commit that `record_head_change` records, or the head this tick's observation read at that commit when a command moves the record out of a state whose observation does not record the head, such as a resume, a `park`, or a replan from a human wait (`controller._landed_due`). In a human wait that holds no operation of that push (every retry died before its upload, or its `uncertain` Result arrived once the wait had begun), the comment becomes due only when such a command moves the record, since an observed head that no write records would make it due again after every post. The comment names the commit the branch holds: discovery that confirmed the operation by its tree and parents (a signed replay) read it under another SHA than the pinned one. An `uncertain` Result alone makes nothing due, since its push may not have landed and the comment says the patch was published at that commit; its operation, once discovery confirms it, or the head, once observed, does. A commit that no `publish_anyway` review pinned, and the reviewed commit of a review that passed or returned the patch for repair, make nothing due. The same tick launches `review-comment.yml` for each due comment (`reconcile.review_comments`), in a concurrency group of its review, so a pending post is replaced only by a later attempt of the same comment. That workflow reads the record from `main` and checks without a token that the comment is still due at the attempt it was launched for, that no pause holds it, that the policy permits writes, and that its development PR is known (`patch_review comment-due`); only then it mints a token for the one fork with `pull-requests: write` alone and posts one comment on the development PR: the latest review, its blocking findings and why it failed, as inert text, each text bounded before it is fenced and the findings that do not fit counted instead, within 60000 characters, ending with a hidden marker that a later attempt finds, on any page of the PR's comments, instead of posting again; only a comment of the App (`riseproject-upstreaming[bot]`) counts (`patch_review comment`). Its receipt (`review-comment.json`) is collected by the controller and folded by the next tick: `posted` or `found` removes the comment; `failed`, `skipped`, or no receipt within 60 minutes is launched again after 30 minutes times the attempts so far, never while the policy is read-only. Once 5 attempts are spent the comment stays with `exhausted` and the last reason: an alert in every tick report says that the development PR lacks it, and it is launched again every 24 hours until it is posted or found, so the alert ends once a human restores what the post needs, up to attempt 999, the last that `review-comment.yml` admits as its `attempt` input (the schema bounds `attempts` the same); after it the comment is no longer launched and the alert says that a human posts it. A post is held, spending no attempt, while a pause holds it or no development PR is recorded: the first patch published anyway has no development PR until `confirm_development_pr` records one, and its comment is launched, at attempt 1, in the first tick after. A pause holds the post of a record that is not terminal, and its resume launches it; a terminal record (`closed.idle`) is never resumed and posting a comment is no development Work, so its pause holds nothing and the comment is posted (`patch_review.held_by_pause`). A held comment is alerted on in every tick once it has been held for 24 hours, and at once on a terminal record, which nothing else will post it for. A record keeps at most 16 due comments: beyond that the oldest exhausted ones are dropped, each with an alert. The record holds at most 32 (the schema's bound), for the comments that become due between two ticks: a comment that becomes due beyond it drops the oldest exhausted comment that is not launched, and only when none is left the oldest other one, never silently: each one dropped is named in the event, the observation, or the controller report of the command that made the other due (`review_comments_dropped`), and the tick alerts on it. Nothing about reviews is posted on the tracking issue; a pass posts nothing. A stop in `development.review_pending`, `development.reviewing` or `development.publishing` discards the held patch and leads to `development.fix_needed`; so does every other exit of those states but the exhaustion of `publish_recovery`, whose wait keeps the held patch until a resume or a refresh discards it.

## CI reruns

The failed or interrupted jobs of the workflow runs on the exact head are rerun by trusted code only, never by a model or a human command, as an operation of kind `ci_rerun` (`ci_rerun.py`). Its marker names each run and the attempt it was at (`ci-rerun:<run>@<attempt>,...`, at most 5 runs), and its receipt is the readback of a higher attempt of every run on the expected head (`ci_rerun.discover`, the discoverer of the kind): a rerun keeps the run id and its creation time, so a launch window cannot confirm it. The run attempt is a monotonic counter, so every request reads it first: a run already above its attempt on the head is a rerun that landed and is confirmed, never requested again, and a `ci_rerun` operation is never left `uncertain`. A run is rerun with `rerun-failed-jobs`, which reruns every failed, cancelled, or timed-out job of that run. The token that requests it is minted for the one verified fork with `actions: write` alone and is used for that request only (the public fork is read with the job's own token); no new workflow exists for it, and the dispatcher has no part in it. A rerun reserves no runner capacity: it is bounded by its per-head budget only, like a push that triggers CI.

There are two paths:

- **A failure not caused by the patch** (`request_ci_rerun`, within `flaky_reruns`). The rerun is the publication of the development worker's `no_change` Result. The trusted publication job of the worker run, and of a publish-only retry, checks without any token (`python -m ci_rerun preflight`) that the Result is a `no_change` Result of development Work (`development_patch` or `development_repair`, the Work of `development.agent_running`) that passes the proposal checks, that `request_ci_rerun` applies to the record (no carried feedback, `flaky_reruns` remaining, not paused), that the policy is not read-only, and that the fork is the recorded one; then it mints the token and runs `python -m ci_rerun publish`, which applies the final check before a fork write (`lifecycle.publication_check`: revision, head, pause, generation, Work id) and reruns the latest attempts of the completed runs on the head that hold a failed job (a publishing job the fork has no credentials for is not rerun). Its outcome:

  | Receipt | Outcome | Row |
  |:--------|:--------|:----|
  | `rerun`: the new attempt of every run read back | `no_change` with the receipt and the confirmed `ci_rerun` operation | `request_ci_rerun` |
  | `superseded`: a run on the head is in progress on a later attempt (a rerun of a run that held a failed job), or the run the recorded evidence names already has a newer attempt (that run is not rerun again), and no other completed run on the head holds a failed job; any other run still in progress does not hold back the completed failed runs, which are rerun, so a retry after a rerun of only some runs reruns the others | `no_change` with the receipt | `request_ci_rerun` |
  | `nothing_to_rerun`: no failed job on the latest attempts | `no_change` with the receipt | `request_ci_rerun`; the same failing evidence is then recorded again by `record_ci_failure`, so the contribution cannot stall |
  | the final check fails, or the run is on another head (`stale`) | `rejected`, failure class `stale` | `replan_development` |
  | a definite refusal (HTTP 403, 404, 422, closing the operation `failed`), a network error, or an attempt not read back after six polls 5 seconds apart | `infra_error`, failure class `infrastructure` | `schedule_publish_retry`, then `exhaust_publish_recovery` |

  A `no_change` outcome without a valid receipt (a worker run on older trusted code, or a publication job that died) is a failed publication when `request_ci_rerun` applies, so the publish-only retry performs the rerun; the outcome counts as `reject_patch_proposal` when the row does not apply. On this path the operation is built from the runs requested and delivered in the outcome, so its intent is not recorded before the request; this is safe because every retry reads the attempt of each run first, and a run already above its attempt on the head is confirmed and never requested again. The reconciler and the preflight admit the same Results: a `no_change` Result of `development_patch` Work (requested when no feedback is pending) is published by the rerun like one of `development_repair` Work, so with nothing failed on the head it takes `request_ci_rerun` with `nothing_to_rerun` and CI is read again in `development.waiting_ci`. The receipt is valid when its status is one of the three answered ones, each run it names carries integer `run_id`, `from_attempt`, and a higher `run_attempt` (at most 5 runs), `superseded` and `nothing_to_rerun` name no run, and, for `rerun`, the confirmed operation names the record's fork, its head, the runs and attempts of its marker, and the attempts of the receipt.

- **Interrupted CI** (`rerun_interrupted_ci`, within `interrupted_reruns`). In `development.waiting_ci` and `handoff.waiting_ci` (`ci_rerun.INTERRUPTED_STATES`), CI on the head is read with the failed jobs of its latest attempts classified (`collect.classify_runner_loss`, at most 20 jobs): a job that failed with a failure-level annotation matching `ci_rerun.RUNNER_LOST` (the runner received a shutdown signal, or a hosted or self-hosted runner lost communication with the server) lost its runner and did not execute. The annotation text is only compared with those constants, never stored or rendered. A required job that is cancelled or lost its runner is interrupted, the verdict is `not_executed`, and the evidence marks the job `interrupted`. A blocking job that no required pattern names and that lost its runner is interrupted too: it is not dropped from the verdict, because the gate, reading the same CI unclassified, would call it failed after a nomination. A `timed_out` job executed and is a failure, and any executed blocking failure gives `failed` and `record_ci_failure`, so a rerun never hides a real failure. For interrupted CI the observation, in order: keeps the state and reads CI again while an operation of the contribution is open or the contribution is paused; takes `exhaust_interrupted_reruns` to `development.waiting_human` with the pending action `ci_interrupted` and the counter `interrupted_reruns` once that budget is spent; and otherwise takes `rerun_interrupted_ci`, which charges `interrupted_reruns` (cause: the evidence id), and records the planned `ci_rerun` operation of the runs that hold an interrupted job (reason ids: the evidence id and `rerun:<count>`) with `active_operation_id` beside the row (`operation_planned`). The controller launches it like a development PR operation, through the development PR workflow, whose `ci_rerun` steps mint the `actions: write` token and run `python -m ci_rerun operation`: it executes only the record's current planned intent (planned, active, same revision, not paused, in `development.waiting_ci`, the recorded fork, a policy that is not read-only) and returns `stale` when the head moved. The workflow uploads the receipt as the `porting-ci-rerun` artifact, which the controller collects (`reconcile --ci-reruns`). The reconciler reads every run of the operation back with its own reads: a confirmed readback closes the operation `confirmed` and releases it (`ci_rerun_recorded`, an event outside the table); a `rerun` receipt not readable yet, including a run that can no longer be read at all (deleted in the fork), is collected again next tick without holding back the rest of the contribution's tick, so the launch budget below closes the operation; a `stale` or `refused` receipt closes the operation `failed` and releases it. The controller also closes a planned `ci_rerun` operation as failed when its launches are spent without a receipt (`operation_launch`, 300 seconds after the last launch) or when it no longer matches the record (another head, revision, or state), or when the contribution is paused: the workflow refuses a paused record, so no receipt can arrive, and an operation left open would hold `park` and a `resume` after `refresh.stale_after_days`. Nothing is launched or planned while the contribution is paused, and the first observation after `resume_pause` takes `rerun_interrupted_ci` again within the budget. No row is fired by a closed rerun: the next observation of interrupted CI takes `rerun_interrupted_ci` again within the budget, or its exhaustion row. `resume_ci` launches nothing itself: it restarts `interrupted_reruns`, and the first observation after it reruns through `rerun_interrupted_ci`, charging the restarted counter.

  In `handoff.waiting_ci` the same rows apply to CI on the clean head (`handoff.ci_head`: the clean head on `riscv64`, or the approved candidate head on `riscv64-dev` when the reviewed series has its tree), with these differences. The operation targets the head the runs are on and its branch (`riscv64` for the clean head, `riscv64-dev` for the candidate head; `ci_rerun.target_ref`). The controller reports its launch in `handoff_requests` (kind `ci_rerun`) instead of `dev_pr_requests`, and `controller.yml` dispatches it to `handoff.yml`, the workflow that writes `riscv64`, whose `ci-rerun` job runs `python -m ci_rerun check --phase handoff` before any token (the checked-out state is the head of `main`, and the operation is the current intent of `handoff.waiting_ci`, planned, active, same revision, not paused, the recorded fork, a policy that is not read-only, and the head and branch of CI on the clean head from the accepted review), then mints the `actions: write` token and runs `python -m ci_rerun operation --phase handoff`. A refused check writes no receipt, because the refusal can be transient: the launch budget launches the operation again, then closes it. `python -m ci_rerun operation --phase handoff` runs the same final check at the head of `main` as the other handoff jobs immediately before the rerun request (`handoff.unchanged_on_main`, see [The handoff operations](#the-handoff-operations)) and, when it refuses, writes no receipt either. The stale fence of the executor is that `riscv64` still resolves to the clean head, the guard of the observation; a moved `riscv64-dev` does not block it. The controller compares the operation with the head of CI on the clean head (`ci_rerun.expected_head`). `rerun_interrupted_ci` and `resume_ci` there also set `clean_head_ci_since`: CI was started again, so the bounded wait of `require_clean_head_ci` starts again, and one incident never needs two human resumes. Interrupted CI takes a row before the bounded wait applies: the wait applies only when no row could be taken (an open operation, a pause, or no completed run that holds an interrupted job). Passing CI on the clean head while an operation of the contribution is open keeps the wait (`accept_deferred`), as development defers nomination; the operation is closed within its launch budget.

In both paths, while a confirmed `ci_rerun` operation of the head recorded a newer attempt of a run than the CI snapshot shows (`ci_rerun.awaited`), the observation reads CI again and acts on nothing; a run the snapshot does not show is not awaited, so the wait cannot stall. CI at the submission gate and in upstream review is read without classification: their verdicts and evidence are unchanged, and only `development.waiting_ci` and `handoff.waiting_ci` rerun interrupted CI.

## Upstream review and release

Upstream review observes the upstream PR the record names (`upstream_pr`), read from the upstream repository, in `upstream_review.waiting_ci` only. The observation takes, in order, the first row that applies:

1. The upstream PR is merged: `record_merge` records `merge` (merge commit, time, target branch, and an evidence record of kind `merge`) and leads to `release_wait.idle`. A merged PR is recorded even when `riscv64` was deleted.
2. It is closed without merge: `record_pr_closed_unmerged` leads to `upstream_review.waiting_human` with the pending action `pr_closed_unmerged`.
3. Its head differs from `upstream_pr.head_sha`: `record_head_change` leads to `upstream_review.waiting_human` with the pending action `upstream_head_changed`, at a new revision, with the new head recorded and the CI evidence cleared. The head a revision of the pipeline published is not such a change: `record_revision_publish_success` recorded it when the Result was folded.
4. An authorized explanation-only comment on the upstream PR takes `record_explanation`: the comment is recorded and quoted on the tracking issue as a code span, so nothing in it renders; nothing is posted on the upstream PR.
5. Authorized actionable review feedback on the upstream PR takes `record_actionable_review` to `upstream_review.fix_needed` at a new revision, with the feedback pending; the observation ends.
6. An executed blocking failure of a CI run on the upstream PR head takes `record_upstream_ci_failure` to `upstream_review.fix_needed`, with the evidence as `last_ci_evidence_id`, once per head and CI run: a failure whose evidence is already recorded is not recorded again, so a resume does not request a revision for it again. Passing CI keeps the state, and CI that is not complete is read again. The pipeline never reruns upstream CI.

Review feedback on the upstream PR is authorized as on the development PR: its author is in the command authority, and it is bound to the recorded head; the ids of the comments a row recorded join the replay fences. `upstream_review.fix_needed` and `upstream_review.agent_running` have no observation row and are not read. `upstream_review.waiting_human` is not observed either, except that with the pending action `pr_closed_unmerged` the upstream PR is read without any change, because its resume requires the PR open again; a parked record that names an upstream PR is read the same way, because `restart_generation` requires that no upstream PR is open. `/upstreaming resume` is refused until that observation finds the PR open again, and `/upstreaming refresh` from `parked.idle` until it finds the PR closed or merged; a failed read establishes nothing and holds no other command back.

Revision Work is allocated in `upstream_review.fix_needed` by `request_upstream_revision` (a failing CI job is recorded for the head; charges `work_total` and the cluster of that job) or `request_review_revision` (feedback only; charges `work_total`), and `exhaust_repair_attempts` leads to `upstream_review.waiting_human` with the exhausted counter once a budget is spent. Its Result is folded in `upstream_review.agent_running` by the rows of [Development](#development) with three differences: a published Result takes `record_revision_publish_success` to `upstream_review.waiting_ci` at a new revision, which clears exactly the feedback its Work carried and records in `upstream_pr.head_sha` the head the publisher read back on `riscv64` (the commit of its confirmed branch operation, or, for a publish-only retry that found the push of an earlier attempt on the branch, the candidate it read there), so the event names the head and the next revision Work is a new Work graph; a published Result that names no head read back is refused; a `no_change` report takes `require_reviewer_answer` to `upstream_review.waiting_human` with the pending action `answer_reviewer` (a human answers the reviewer on the upstream PR; the pipeline posts nothing there, and `review_response.py` answers nothing for a record in upstream review); and a proposal rejected as stale takes `reject_patch_proposal`, as every other rejection there. An uncertain Result (its push was not read back) is a failed publication, as in development, and the publish-only retry reads back the head a lost push left without pushing again. Publish-only retries run in `upstream_review.agent_running` as in development. Every exhaustion leads to `upstream_review.waiting_human`; nothing parks from upstream review without `/upstreaming park`, which applies in `upstream_review.waiting_human` only. `resume_upstream_review` (after `pr_closed_unmerged`, `answer_reviewer`, or `upstream_head_changed`) returns to `upstream_review.waiting_ci` without Work and clears the pending feedback; `resume_upstream_revision` (after failed revision Work or an exhausted budget) returns to `upstream_review.fix_needed`.

In `release_wait.idle` the official release is observed at most every 60 minutes (`release.RELEASE_POLL_MINUTES`, recorded in `next_poll_at`). The acceptance contract is the target contract the record names (`target_contract_id`): its distributions and the matrix rows (`release_rows`: interpreter, ABI, libc family, and platform floor) their official release must cover. `release.decide` takes one row:

- `require_release_intervention` to `release_wait.waiting_human` when no such contract is recorded (pending action `release_contract_missing`) or PyPI has no such distribution (`release_distribution_missing`);
- `verify_release` to `released.idle` when, for every distribution, the oldest official release that was uploaded after the merge and whose tag (`v<version>` or `<version>`) contains the merge commit covers every row with non-yanked wheels (`release.coverage`); it records `release_acceptance` and an evidence record of kind `release_acceptance`;
- `record_release_incomplete` otherwise, recorded once per distinct observation (the event's `observation_key`).

A failed read of PyPI or of GitHub is a collector failure: nothing changes, and the next tick reads again. `resume_release_observation` returns to `release_wait.idle`.

## The plan decides

A planning run Result is folded in `planning.agent_running` only, through the table's rows. The plan is recorded as evidence first; then exactly one row is taken:

| Plan | Row | Leads to |
|:--------|:----|:---------|
| None, a decision that is none of develop, park, and close, or a decision close that PyPI does not confirm (including a PyPI project that does not name the upstream repository) | `reject_plan`, or `exhaust_planning_recovery` once `planning_attempts` is spent | `planning.idle` at a new revision (a new planning run is due), or `planning.waiting_human` |
| Develop, and a refresh recorded that began with the fork and development PR the record still holds | `readmit_development` | `development.fix_needed` on the recorded fork, at a new revision |
| Develop otherwise, with a complete patch scope | `admit_development` | `provisioning.idle` at a new revision; the fork is provisioned before any patch Work |
| Develop otherwise, with an incomplete patch scope | `reject_plan` (or `exhaust_planning_recovery`) | as for an invalid plan |
| Park, with a recorded `admission_override` that names a class and paths | `admit_development`, or `readmit_development` as for develop | as for develop, with the override's class and paths |
| Park, with an override that names no class or no paths | `reject_plan` (or `exhaust_planning_recovery`) | as for an invalid plan |
| Park, without an override | `park_planning` | `parked.idle`, with the plan's rationale as `parked_reason`; the generation ends without a revision bump |
| Close, and PyPI confirms it | `close_planning` | `closed.idle` with `terminal_reason` `already_released` (a riscv64 wheel of the latest release is on PyPI) or `no_work` (pure-Python wheels only); a recorded override is not applied, and the event and the tracking-issue entry say so |
| Close, and PyPI does not confirm it | `reject_plan` (or `exhaust_planning_recovery`) | as for an invalid plan |

The patch scope is a guard of `admit_development` (the fact "the verified plan names `scope_paths`, and `scope_coverage_complete` holds"): the reconciler establishes it when the admitted scope has an admission class, names 1 to 32 safe scope paths, and matches every path the plan's `path_coverage` lists as possibly needing a riscv64 change (`plan_gate.scope_problem`). For an override the paths are the human's scope: the class and the paths are required, and the coverage is recorded but does not refuse it. `readmit_development` has no scope guard. Both admissions record the plan, the admission class, and the scope paths, clear the refresh and the override, and record beside the row in `scope_coverage_complete` whether the coverage was complete for the admitted scope; repair Work reads it. A rejected plan keeps the override and the refresh for the next planning.

A decision close ends the contribution for good, so the plan's word alone never closes it: the reconciler reads PyPI itself (`collect.pypi_project`) for every distribution the record names and confirms the close (`plan_gate.close_confirmation`, the fact "PyPI serves riscv64 for the latest release of every distribution, and each PyPI project names the upstream repository") when each PyPI project names the record's upstream GitHub repository in its metadata (a project URL, the home page, or the download URL, compared without case and without `.git`) and the latest release of each publishes a non-yanked riscv64 wheel (`linux_riscv64`, `manylinux_*_riscv64`, or `musllinux_*_riscv64`, any interpreter), or pure-Python (platform `any`) wheels only. The reconciler derives `terminal_reason` itself, never from the plan: `already_released` when at least one distribution has a riscv64 wheel, otherwise `no_work`. A missing PyPI project, a PyPI project that does not name the upstream repository (the recorded packages may be names of any registry, and a PyPI project of the same name from another repository confirms nothing), an sdist-only release, platform wheels without riscv64, and an empty or invalid list of distributions do not confirm it, and the plan is rejected. PyPI is the one registry a close is confirmed from: the trusted research gate already refuses a close whose plan records no PyPI release channel, a release channel without riscv64, or no recorded package, so a project of another ecosystem that already serves riscv64 is parked with that evidence. A failed PyPI read is a collector failure: the contribution is skipped for the tick with no state change, and the same Result is folded at the next tick. The tracking-issue entry of the close shows the release and one wheel PyPI serves, quoted; the plan's rationale stays in the event.

## Errors while a tick runs

An error in one contribution does not stop the controller tick. Every step of a tick that works on one contribution runs inside `isolation.contribution(contribution_id, step)`: the confirmation of a development PR or provisioning receipt, the fold of a worker Result, dispatch recovery, the recovery of recorded review feedback, the observation of the fork branch and CI, the canary recovery, the intake-label check, the source identity check, the application of a command, the discovery alerts of an operation, the provisioning and development PR plans, Work selection, the publish-only retry, the history of requested Work (`work_history`), and the check of the operations the tick stages. An exception that leaves such a block is attributed to that contribution and classified by its class:

| Exception | Class | What the tick does |
|:----------|:------|:-------------------|
| `collect.CollectError`, `http.client.HTTPException`, `OSError` | Collector failure: GitHub could not be read for the contribution. | The contribution is skipped for the tick: no state change, no pause. The report lists it under `skipped_contributions` with the step, the error class, and the message. |
| Every other exception, including `lifecycle.TransitionError` and `schemas.SchemaError` raised outside the application of a command | Unexpected error (`isolation.ProcessingError`). | Everything staged for the contribution is discarded, none of its effects is requested, the other contributions are processed, and the contribution takes `pause_on_processing_error`. |

Development happens on `riscv64-dev`. A record in the `development`, `handoff`, `upstream_review`, or `release_wait` phase whose fork names another development branch (`fork.head_ref`) is a shape no row of the table handles: the first step of the tick (`record_shape`) raises a `TransitionError` for it, so it takes `pause_on_processing_error` and nothing is done for it, and a paused one is left as it is. The pipeline has no migration for such a record.

Every GitHub read of the collector goes through `collect.Api`, which turns each failure to read a response into `collect.CollectError` with a bounded message: an HTTP error status, an error of the HTTP layer (`http.client.HTTPException`, for example `IncompleteRead` or `RemoteDisconnected`), an error of the transport (`OSError`, which covers `urllib.error.URLError`, TLS errors such as `ssl.SSLEOFError`, socket timeouts, and reset connections), and a response body that is not JSON (`json.JSONDecodeError`, `UnicodeDecodeError`), for example one cut off in transit. The body itself is never quoted. Only the read and the decoding are converted: a `KeyError`, `TypeError`, or `ValueError` raised while a well-formed response is used is an unexpected error. The HTTP-layer and transport classes are also in the collector class above, as a safety net for a read that does not go through `collect.Api`.

Expected rejections never reach the block and pause nothing, as before: a `TransitionError` raised while a command is applied and a command the table refuses are recorded as a rejected command with the reason, a command that fails authentication or the grammar is recorded as rejected, and an intake form that raises `intake.IntakeError` is reported as invalid. A malformed development PR receipt (`KeyError`, `ValueError`) is ignored until a valid one arrives.

Staging is all or nothing per contribution. `reconcile.tick` computes the tick on a copy of the committed state. When a contribution fails, the tick is computed again from the committed state with that contribution left out: no step processes it, so no record, event, operation, evidence, or command receipt is staged for it and no Work, dispatcher launch, provisioning, development PR, publication, or publish retry is requested for it. It still counts as an existing contribution for the others: it keeps its worker slot and its upstream. A command posted on its tracking issue, `resume` included, is left unconsumed and gets no reply: it is read again by every tick until one processes the contribution, and is applied or rejected then. A request that would become a new contribution in this tick is deferred and read again by the next tick. Before the result is returned, the tick verifies that nothing staged and nothing requested names the contribution, and stops otherwise.

After an unexpected error the controller takes `pause_on_processing_error` on the record as it was committed before the tick, through the engine. The verb sets `paused` with `paused_by` `controller` and `paused_at`, clears the requested Work and the pending publication, and keeps the revision; from an `agent_running` state it leads to the state in which the Work is due again; `paused_reason` is `processing_error: <error class>: <message> (step <step>, run <run id>)`. The record and the event are the only things the tick stages for the contribution. The event carries the step, the error class, the message, and the run id in its `details`; the status ledger posts them on the tracking issue with the statement that everything else of that run was discarded. The report lists the error under `controller.alerts` and, with its fields, under `processing_errors`. `python -m reconcile` still exits with status 0, since the tick goes on, and prints one GitHub Actions `::error::` annotation per processing error, built from the same bounded text and escaped as the workflow command format requires, so that a pipeline bug charged to a contribution is visible on the workflow run. `/upstreaming resume` at the current revision clears the pause and bumps the revision.

The error text is bounded and sanitized before it is stored or posted (`isolation.clean_text`): the text normalization of the state store is applied, everything that matches a credential pattern of the store is replaced, control characters and line breaks become one space, and the message is cut to 300 characters. The traceback is written to the log of the workflow run only.

A contribution that is already stopped is not paused again. The verb is guarded by `not paused`, like every verb that sets the flag, so an error in a contribution that is paused, by a human or by the controller, changes nothing and appends no event, whatever the error is; the same holds for a terminal contribution. Observations still fold while a contribution is paused, so an error that persists is met again by every tick: each tick leaves the contribution out, lists the error in its report, and writes nothing. The next error after a resume pauses it again. An error in a contribution that is already stopped, and in a contribution that the same tick would have created from an intake request, which has no committed record to pause, is reported only in the tick report (`controller.alerts` and `processing_errors`) and in the workflow annotation: no event is appended and nothing is posted on the tracking issue.

Stop commands are applied before any Work is selected. When applying `pause` or `park` fails with an unexpected error, the contribution is errored like in any other step: the tick is computed again without it, so nothing is launched for it, and it is paused by the controller.

The whole tick stops, nothing is committed, and no contribution is paused when:

- unexpected errors occur in two or more contributions in one tick. Collector failures do not count: any number of contributions can be skipped. Neither do pauses that trusted code requests for one contribution (`isolation.PauseRequest`, such as an accepted Result whose report cannot be stored, `worker_report.ReportError`): each is handled like an unexpected error of its contribution (`isolation.ContributionPause`), and any number of them in one tick pause their contributions and leave the tick going;
- `pause_on_processing_error` cannot be applied to the errored contribution, for example because its record is in no admitted state;
- an exception is raised outside every block: the state load and the validation of the store, the policy, the text and record migrations, the collection of the tick's inputs (intake issues, command comments, tracking issues, source snapshots), command authentication, the intake of a request that is not a contribution yet, a command on an issue that tracks no contribution, the staging of the result into the store, and the state commit.

`python -m reconcile` exits with status 1 and a message that names the contributions and their errors in the first two cases, and with the error itself in the third, as before. The record migration is in the third group although it works on one record: a record that awaits the migration is not in a state of the table, so no row could pause it, and the state load refuses a handoff record in any other shape.

## Worker reports, history and steering

Every worker run of a patch, repair, revision, submission rewrite or submission review writes a report (`report.json`, `schemas/worker_report.schema.json`) beside its Result: a summary, what was attempted and why, each approach with its outcome (`adopted`, `abandoned`, `failed`, `inconclusive`), its evidence and its lesson, the failures with their evidence, what remains open, the verification done, and the earlier failed or abandoned approaches it tried again with the new evidence that justified it. The report is untrusted text. When the Work payload carries a history (`context.history`), the trusted gate requires a report that names the contribution, the revision, the task, and the digest of that history (`worker_report.check`): the worker gate rejects a patch Result that is otherwise accepted without one (`reject_patch_proposal`), and the submission checks treat a rewrite or review without one as Work without a usable Result. The worker gate checks the report last, after it classified the Result: a blocked Result is `worker_blocked` whatever its report, since that report is discarded anyway. Work dispatched before reports existed may finish without one. Planning writes no report: the verified plan is its report.

The report is stored only for a successful worker, one whose own Result is accepted and acted on, whatever CI says afterwards. The reconciler stores it as `worker_report` evidence (`report-<contribution>-r<revision>-<worker run>`, an id over 128 characters keeping its first 111 and 16 hex digits of its SHA-256, like the `steering-<contribution>-<digest>` ids; an id that still cannot be stored is a report that cannot be stored), bound to the Work id, generation, revision and task and the time the Work was requested, with the head before and after the Work and the changed files of the trusted operation, when one of these rows folds the Result (`reconcile.REPORT_VERBS`): `record_publish_success`, `record_revision_publish_success`, `hold_patch_for_review`, `accept_submission_rewrite`, `accept_submission_review`, `request_ci_rerun`, and `require_reviewer_answer`. The report of a development patch is stored at its hold, whatever its review then decides, so the review and every later Work read it; it records the commit the patch rebuilds to as `head_candidate` until the publication of the reviewed patch stores it again with that commit as `head_after`, under the revision of the Work that wrote it. The event of the row names the evidence (`report_evidence_id`), or records (`report: absent`) that Work dispatched without a history returned no usable report. A success row never drops the report of Work that carries a history: the gate accepted that Result only with a valid report, so when the reconciler finds it missing, unreadable or invalid, or cannot build its evidence, `worker_report.evidence` raises inside the contribution's isolation block and the tick takes `pause_on_processing_error` instead of the row, whose reason names the Work and why its report cannot be stored. That pause is specific to the contribution: any number of them in one tick stop nothing else. The pause clears the Work, so the Result is never folded without its report: it takes `record_late_effect` the next time it is seen, which records it as processed (the artifact is not acted on again) and records in the event that its report could not be stored, and a branch effect it carried is seen by head observation after the resume. A Result that takes `record_late_effect` although its push landed (status `published` with a head read back, for example a Result of a revision the record has left) is the work of a successful worker whose head is on the branch, and CI runs on that head: its report is stored as `worker_report` evidence of the Work it answers (verb `record_late_effect`, head after the Work as for the success row of its task), named in the event, so a repair after failing CI on that head reads it. A late Result whose push was not read back (status `uncertain`) may have landed: its report is stored the same way with the commit it proposed as `head_candidate` and no head after it; a report that cannot be stored there is recorded in the event (`report: not stored: ...`) and changes nothing else. A Result whose push may have landed but that no success row folds stores its report too (`reconcile.pushed_report_evidence`), with the commit it proposed as `head_candidate`: an `uncertain` Result held for a publish-only retry stores it at the hold (verb `schedule_publish_retry`, or `exhaust_publish_recovery` when the budget is spent), since the Result is then marked processed and its artifact never read again, whatever ends the hold (a retry, the exhaustion of the retries, a pause, a controller stop, a park); a publish-only retry rejected as stale while the operation of the earlier attempt is still open (`replan_development`), or, in upstream review, behind a confirmed one (`reject_patch_proposal`), stores it with the commit of that operation. The success fold of a retry of the same worker run stores the same evidence id again with the head after the Work, so no report is stored twice. Like a success row, a report the gate accepted that cannot be stored there pauses the contribution. Every other fold (a rejection, a blocked worker, a replan, a failed rewrite or review verdict, an unusable submission Result, an `infra_error` publication, a late Result that published nothing) discards the report with its artifact; the rejection reasons of the trusted gate are not fed forward either. The head after the Work is the commit of the confirmed publication operation the row names: the Result's own, or, for a publish-only retry that read the lost push back or was rejected as stale behind it, the confirmed operation of the earlier attempt (`worker_report.head_after`); else the candidate a publish-only retry found, or the head a revision read back. A report whose push was not read back (a held or late `uncertain` Result, a stale retry behind the operation of an earlier attempt, or a development patch held for its review) records the commit it proposed as `head_candidate` (`worker_report.head_candidate`) and no head after it: the history matches it only to that very commit, so a CI run on that commit is linked to it and it is the report of the head only while the recorded head is that commit. A report file is bounded by `worker_report.MAX_REPORT_BYTES` (32 KiB) as written, in the artifact and at the gate; the worker's instructions and the schema state that limit and that a larger report is refused with the Result, and the schema bounds each list and field so that a valid report cannot greatly exceed it. The reconciler reads the report file of the publication artifact exactly as the gate read it (`worker_report.read`: a regular file, at most one byte over the bound) and checks those bytes again, so a report accepted at the gate is never refused at the fold. A document that is not JSON, or is nested deeper than the parser allows, is an invalid report. A lone surrogate (an escape such as `\ud800`) is dropped from the stored text, since the store cannot encode it. No record field changes.

Human steering is collected when Work is allocated (`collect.steering`): the tracking issue description when it differs from the description frozen at intake and the issue author is in the command authority, the tracking issue comments, and the reviews, review comments and conversation comments of the development PR and of the upstream PR, each list bounded to its newest five pages of 100 (review comments sorted newest first by the API, every other list read from its last page backwards through the `Link` header), so the newest text is the text read. A review with an empty body is kept when its state is a verdict (`APPROVED`, `CHANGES_REQUESTED`, `DISMISSED`); since GitHub gives an edited review no new time, a review's version is its submission time and the digest of its state and body, so an edited or dismissed review is a new version. A list with more pages than were read (a `rel=next` link left after the fifth page read, or a `rel=last` link past the fifth page, whatever the number of items on its last page) holds older items that were not read (an earlier collection read them while the list was shorter, unless it grew past the bound between two collections); it is flagged in `omitted` (`<source>_list_cap_reached`), recorded once in a `human_steering` record even when nothing else is new, and shown in the history. Only accounts whose immutable id is in `command_authority.actor_ids` steer (`authority` true). The REST API does not name the last editor of a text, and the collector reads no GraphQL. On the control repository and the fork, write access is part of the command authority's trust: the edited tracking issue description is attributed to the issue author and trusted by that author's authority, and an edited comment, review comment or review of an authority account steers like any other, also when it was edited before it was first collected. On the upstream PR, whose maintainers can edit those texts and are not in the command authority (`collect.edits_unverified`), a comment or review comment of an authority account that was edited after it was written (`updated_at` differs from `created_at`), and a new version of such an account's review (its state or body changed), is recorded with `authority` false and `edit_unverified` true: the edited text is shown in the timeline as an `unverified_edit` entry and never steers; the version recorded before the edit still steers. On the upstream PR, accounts whose association is `OWNER`, `MEMBER` or `COLLABORATOR` are kept as maintainer review feedback (`authority` false), never as steering; every other account, every bot and every other empty body is counted per source and not recorded. New items are recorded as `human_steering` evidence with author, times and link (`steering.new_evidence`): an edited comment is a new version that names the one it supersedes, and a deleted comment stays recorded. One record holds at most 200 items (180 KB). What the histories of one tick stage is bounded in bytes, so the state commit always fits `storage.max_commit_bytes` with the rest of the tick: at most 256 KiB of new `human_steering` records per contribution (`reconcile.STEERING_BYTES_PER_CONTRIBUTION`), recorded newest first so that the history holds the newest steering, and at most 1 MiB of steering and histories per tick (`reconcile.HISTORY_BYTES_PER_TICK`). Items left out are recorded by the next tick that builds a history for the contribution and are counted meanwhile in the history's `omitted` as `steering_deferred` and in the tick report; a Work whose history does not fit the bound of the tick is listed in the report and gets its history from a later tick, while the dispatcher defers it. The history's `omitted` counts are those of the newest collection, not a sum over collections. Steering fires no row, never sets `review_scope_extension`, and never changes `pending_feedback_evidence_ids`, `scope_paths`, or any other field; the human relay and inquiry rows of the observation are unchanged. The frozen intake description stays the request identity and the plan's digest.

The tick builds the history of every requested Work that has none (`work_history.build`), after the publish-only retries and only in a tick that observes: for the record as the tick leaves it, normally in the same commit as the allocation, and for any requested Work that has no history yet. The history is fixed when it is built, since the dispatcher pins it in the payload of that Work id: steering written after that, while the same Work waits for a worker slot or is dispatched again (`retry_dispatch`, or the queue's retry after a worker run that ended without a Result), reaches only the next Work. A history recorded after its Work was submitted (Work requested before the history existed) reaches only the next Work too: the queued payload, which names no history, is never replaced. A Work re-armed after a refused dispatch (`rearm_dispatch`), resumed after a human wait, or retried after a terminal graph is allocated at a new revision, so it has a new Work id and a new history, built with the steering written during the wait. Its id is derived from the Work id (`history-<32 hex>`), so `active_work` needs no new field. The history is a pure function of the evidence of the contribution, all generations included: the steering items of the command authority in the order they were written, then one chronological timeline of the stored worker reports, the CI runs (each linked by `after_report` to the report whose published head it ran on, so a repair after failing CI reads what the passing worker tried), every review of a held development patch (`patch_review`: its verdict, what became of the patch, its summary and findings, and the answers to earlier findings, linked by `report_evidence_id` to the report of the patch it reviewed; its short form keeps the verdict and each finding's severity, file and title), the review feedback and inquiries the observation recorded, the submission rewrite and review verdicts, and the reviews of upstream maintainers. A report stored by `record_late_effect` is placed at the time its Work was requested (`work.requested_at` of the report, which the payload carries; `stored_at` names when it was stored), never as the newest entry by the time it was stored. It is at most 64 KiB in canonical JSON, and every bound of it is in bytes of canonical JSON, so multibyte text counts as what it costs. A larger history is condensed deterministically, never dropped uncounted, and its size is checked after every step. Two things are protected. The reports (`work_history.protected_reports`): the newest report, by the Work it describes (its generation and revision, then its time), and the report of the head, the newest report whose `head_after`, or `head_candidate` (the commit of a push that was not read back), is the recorded head of the branch the record works on (the head CI ran on), else the newest report that published a head, together with the newest CI run of each workflow linked to it (at most 8); a report that published nothing (a CI rerun, a reviewer answer, a submission rewrite or review) is never the report of the head, so a repair after failing CI reads the whole report of the worker whose head failed whatever was stored after it. And the steering of the owner, whose newest items keep a reserve of 16 KiB. Everything else gives way first: (1) the other timeline entries (older reports and their CI runs, further CI runs, reviews of development patches, review feedback, submission verdicts, maintainer feedback) become a short form, oldest first (the worker's summary and each approach's title, outcome and lesson as separate fields, a CI state and its first failing jobs, the first 300 bytes of feedback); (2) the oldest steering items beyond the reserve are cut to 300 bytes; (3) the other timeline entries are folded, oldest first, into one counted `earlier` entry (those before the protected report that comes last) or one counted `later` entry (those after it), with counts per kind, times, evidence ids and the title and outcome of each failed or abandoned approach (each list bounded to 50, with the number left out stated); (4) the protected reports take their reduced form (`reduced`, with `evidence_path`): every section kept, each field cut to a bound (the summary to 1000 bytes, each approach's title, outcome, lesson, evidence and description, each failure, open item and revisited entry, and shorter what was attempted and verified), so no steering item is folded while a protected report is whole; (5) the oldest steering items beyond the reserve are folded into one counted `steering_earlier` entry (times and evidence ids); (6) every steering item but the newest is cut short and then folded, oldest first; (7) only then are the linked CI runs, the newest steering item, the newest report and, last of all, the report of the head condensed and folded. A fully folded history always fits its bound; should it not, its lists of evidence ids are shortened with the number left out stated (`condensed.degraded`) rather than the Work held. `sources` lists at most the 100 newest source evidence ids and `sources_not_listed` counts the rest. The condensation keeps the size of the document up to date by the size change of each step and serializes the whole document only when it may fit, so its cost grows linearly with the evidence. A review feedback entry lists at most the 60 newest items of its record (20 once condensed) and counts the rest (`items_not_listed`). A review feedback item whose text is in the steering section is replaced by a pointer to it only when it is the same kind of GitHub object (review, review comment or comment) with the same id. Every cut passes through `worker_report.clean`, and the built record is checked for the credential patterns of the store and redacted again (`worker_report.scrub`), since a cut or a join of clean text can create a match; a record that still matches raises inside the contribution's isolation block, so it pauses that contribution and never stops the tick. Entries with the same time are ordered with the worker report first, and a CI run is linked to the report that published its head whatever their order, so the CI observed in the tick that folds the Result is linked to that Result's report. A condensed entry, of the timeline or of the steering, names the evidence file that holds it in full (`evidence_path`). A steering list that GitHub answers with 404, 410 or 451 (a deleted fork, an upstream repository or pull request that is gone or not accessible to the App) is one no retry will read: it is named in `steering_unreadable` and in a limitation of the history, and the Work proceeds with what could be read. Any other failed GitHub read while the steering is collected (a server error, a rate limit, a 403, a transport error) defers only the history: the allocation and the rest of the tick are committed, the entry of the Work in the tick report names the failure and how long the Work has waited (`deferred`), the dispatcher defers the Work, and each later tick reads the lists again. The grace is measured from the committed `active_work.requested_at`, which a deferral never moves: once the Work has waited an hour (`reconcile.STEERING_FAILURE_GRACE`), a list that still fails is read without: it is named in `steering_unreadable` like a list that is gone, what failed is reported under `steering_failed` and in `controller.alerts` (the HTTP status GitHub answered, or `no HTTP answer` and the error for a read GitHub never answered), and the history is built, so one failing list never holds the contribution. A list read for its newest pages whose first page names no last page is read forwards, keeping its five newest pages, for at most 50 pages (`collect.NEWEST_SCAN_PAGES`); a longer one is a failed read, never its oldest pages. A list page GitHub answers with an empty body is a failed read too (`CollectError`), never a pause.

The dispatcher (`python -m work plan`) gives every Work, planning included, its history by reference (`context.history`: the control repository, the pinned commit, the path, the SHA-256, and the evidence id; no URL, to keep the payload small), like the plan. Work whose history is not committed yet is not dispatched in that run and is listed under `deferred`. Dispatch recovery decides nothing for it while its history is not committed, for at most two hours from the request (`reconcile.HISTORY_WAIT`, the steering grace and a margin): nothing is charged and nothing is launched. After that it is decided like any other, so a history that is never recorded ends in the human wait of `exhaust_dispatch_recovery` after `retry_dispatch`. Once the history is committed, the dispatcher runs are counted from the time it was recorded; the tick that records the history of Work an earlier tick requested asks for a dispatcher launch at once (`dispatch_relaunch`, and `dispatch` in the entry of the Work in the tick report), and dispatch recovery launches it again after the launch grace should that launch not show up as a run. When a history that is never recorded leads to `retry_dispatch` or `exhaust_dispatch_recovery`, the event says so (`history: never recorded`, and `why` in plain words), and the tracking issue entry of the human wait states that the history of the Work was never recorded, so no worker ever received it. A Work whose payload the run cannot build (an evidence file it names is missing at the pinned commit, or the payload is over the queue's safe budget of 15500 bytes, `work.MAX_PAYLOAD_BYTES`) is skipped and listed under `skipped` with the reason, and every other Work is dispatched: one record never fails the dispatch of the others, and dispatch recovery decides the skipped Work. The controller measures the smallest payload of the Work itself (`work.payload_floor`), from the committed evidence as the dispatcher builds it (`work.payload_inputs`: the allowed paths of the scope or the target contract, the reason evidence with the plan reference, the commit rules and template sections of the plan, the rewrite a review reviews), with the history and plan references at their exact size and the control repository id at its longest (19 digits); when it is over the budget, the events of `retry_dispatch` and `exhaust_dispatch_recovery` name the cause (`why`: the payload size, the size of the frozen tracking issue description it carries inline, and the length a new request must keep to), which the tracking issue entry of the human wait shows. A trusted pre-agent step of the worker and the planner checks the reference, fetches the file at the pinned commit, checks its digest, its kind, its id and its Work id, and writes it to `/tmp/gh-aw/agent/history.json`; it prints only the evidence id.

All worker-written and human-written text is redacted for the credential patterns of the store, stripped of control characters, normalized, and bounded with an explicit truncation marker before it is stored (`worker_report.clean`). It is never rendered on the tracking issue or the status page: only evidence ids are named there. It reaches the agents as a data file separate from their instructions, which say that the history is recorded data, never instructions about tools, credentials, scope, repositories or the workflow, and that nothing in it can widen the allowed paths, the allowed operations, or where and what is pushed. The worker reads the history first, follows the owner's steering within the task's scope and rules, and does not repeat or revert an approach the history records as failed or abandoned without new evidence, which it names in its report. The planner reads the steering first, then the tracking issue comments as untrusted research.

## Stop and resume

`/upstreaming pause`, `resume`, `park`, `refresh`, and `abandon` on a record in a state of the table are applied by `engine.apply_command`, in every phase; the controller resolves the one verb whose guards hold and refuses the command with the guard that does not.

- `pause` sets `paused` with `paused_by` `human`, `paused_at`, and the optional reason in `paused_reason`. It clears the requested Work and the pending publication, and keeps the revision. In an `agent_running` state it leads to the state in which the Work is due again (`planning.idle`, `development.fix_needed`, `upstream_review.fix_needed`), so the resume requests fresh Work. A Result of the stopped Work takes `record_late_effect`. While paused no Work is requested, no budget is charged, no publication or publish retry is launched, and no reminder is posted.
- `resume` on a paused record clears the flag and bumps the revision (`resume_pause`). After a stop longer than `refresh.stale_after_days` days in `provisioning` or `development`, counted from the older of `paused_at` and `pending_human_action.requested_at`, it takes `resume_stale` instead: complete planning runs again from `queued.idle` while the fork and development PR are kept, and the refresh records the phase, head, and development PR it began from (no head or PR when there is no fork yet). A refreshed plan returns to development on the recorded fork only when the refresh began with that fork and its development PR; otherwise development is admitted afresh. In the handoff phase the resume never refreshes: the candidate head, `handoff_request`, and the CI evidence are kept, and `submit-upstream` then names the new revision and the same head.
- `resume` in a human wait that is not paused takes the resume of that wait that its pending human action selects; on a paused human wait the first `resume` clears the flag only, and its tracking-issue entry names the pending action that remains, so a second `resume` leaves the wait.
- `refresh` in `parked.idle` starts a new generation at `queued.idle` (`restart_generation`); it requires the exact revision, no open operation and no pending publication, and no open upstream PR, and it is accepted while paused: the new generation then stays paused until a resume. It accepts an optional admission class and paths, `/upstreaming refresh revision=N class=<class> paths="<glob>,<glob>" reason="..."`, which are recorded with the reason as `admission_override`; the paths must be safe relative paths, as for a scope. The class and the paths are given together or not at all: an override admits development only with both, so a refresh that names only one of them is refused with that reason and changes nothing. The new generation still runs complete planning; a plan that decides park then admits development with the override's class and paths, and a plan that decides develop or close is applied as it decides (see [The plan decides](#the-plan-decides)). An optional `branch=NAME` names the upstream target branch of the new generation instead of the one the request asked for at intake, `/upstreaming refresh revision=N branch=NAME reason="..."`: the way to continue on a branch the upstream renamed or replaced (see [The upstream base branch](#the-upstream-base-branch)); a name that is not a safe Git branch name is refused. `class`, `paths`, and `branch` are refused by every other refresh, and `head` by the refresh in `parked.idle`.
- `resume` in `parked.idle` only ends a pause (`resume_pause`). On a contribution there that is not paused it is refused with the reply that a new generation starts with `/upstreaming refresh revision=N reason="..."` and that `class=` and `paths=` on it record an admission override; a resume that names `class` or `paths` is rejected when it is parsed, with the same refresh form.
- `abandon` applies in `parked.idle` only, with a reason and at any revision, also while paused. It leads to `closed.idle` with `terminal_reason` `abandoned`; the reason the human gave is kept in the event. A closed contribution is final: no command resumes it. Elsewhere the command is rejected with the statement that it applies in `parked.idle`; park first.
- Besides `parked.idle` (above), `refresh` applies in the states whose rows take `refresh_planning`: `development.fix_needed`, `development.waiting_ci`, `development.waiting_human`, `handoff.awaiting_submission`, and `handoff.waiting_upstream_pr`, with the exact revision, no requested Work, no open operation or pending publication, and a recorded development PR and head; it is accepted while paused. In `handoff.waiting_upstream_pr` it names the exact clean head, `/upstreaming refresh revision=N head=<clean head> reason="..."`; elsewhere a `head` is refused.
- `park` takes `park_contribution` from every state the rule covers, with a reason and with no open operation; it is accepted while paused. It does not apply in `handoff.waiting_upstream_pr`, `release_wait.*`, and the `upstream_review` states other than `upstream_review.waiting_human`.

A stop command is honored at a stale revision; every other command names the current revision. The reply to a `pause` of a paused contribution says it is already paused, and the reply to a `resume` of a contribution that is neither paused nor waiting says it is not paused.

`/upstreaming block` is retired: the lifecycle has one stop flag, `paused`, with its reason and who set it. The command is rejected with a message that names `pause`. The controller sets the same flag when the upstream repository identity changes (`pause_on_identity_change`, `paused_by` `controller`, `paused_reason` `upstream_identity_changed`) and after a processing error.

## Commands

The grammar (`commands.GRAMMAR`) has the commands of the table and two intake commands:

| Command | Applies in |
|:--------|:-----------|
| `pause`, `resume`, `park`, `refresh`, `abandon`, `submit-upstream`, `retract-pr` | The states whose rows, or the `paused` flag, take them; see [Stop and resume](#stop-and-resume) and [Requesting upstream submission](#requesting-upstream-submission). |
| `authorize-followup` | A merged contribution (`release_wait`, `released`); it records a follow-up request. Outside the table. |
| `resolve-operation` | An operation, by its id, with the disposition and a `reason`, posted on the tracking issue of the operation's contribution; see the operation table. Outside the table. |

The retired commands are rejected with a message that names their replacement, and the reply on the tracking issue shows the replacement's form:

| Retired | Replacement |
|:--------|:------------|
| `request-handoff`, `approve-handoff`, `register-upstream-pr` | `submit-upstream` at the submission gate; the upstream PR is verified by observation. |
| `block` | `pause`, with a reason. |
| `approve-scope` | None needed: the verified plan decides admission and provisioning starts automatically. A parked contribution restarts from `parked.idle` with `refresh`, with `class` and `paths` as an admission override. |
| `provision` | None needed: the fork is provisioned automatically. In `provisioning.waiting_human`, correct the prerequisite, then `resume`; the resume states that the prerequisite is corrected. |
| `restart` | `refresh` from `parked.idle`; `closed.idle` is final. |
| `clarify` | `resume` in the human wait once the cause is corrected. |
| `revise-scope` | `refresh`, in `parked.idle` with `class` and `paths`. |
| `force-develop` | `refresh` from `parked.idle` with `class` and `paths`: the admission override that a plan decision park then applies. |

The tracking-issue entries offer, under "Possible next commands", the commands the table takes in the contribution's state (in `parked.idle` also the refresh with an admission override, prefilled with the recorded class and paths, also while paused; a resume is offered there only to end a pause), for every state. A retired command is never offered.

## Tracking issue entries and the status page

The status ledger (`status_ledger.publish`) posts an App-authored entry on the tracking issue only for an event that needs a human, and the lifecycle table decides which those are:

- the event enters a state of kind `gate`, `human_wait`, or `locked` (the kinds of `lifecycle.REMINDER_KINDS`, which name a pending human step), or keeps the contribution in such a state at a new revision, since the commands a human posts name the revision (the first `resume` of a paused human wait is one);
- the event sets the stop flag (`paused` is `true` in the event's `to`): `pause`, `pause_on_identity_change`, and `pause_on_processing_error`;
- the event enters a state of kind `generation_end` (`parked.idle`) or `terminal` (`closed.idle`, with an abandon, a withdrawal, or a close, and `released.idle`);
- the event is a reminder of a pending human step: the rule that applies in exactly the states of those kinds and keeps the state (`record_reminder`);
- the event is a wait for an unavailable upstream (`status_ledger.UNAVAILABLE_KINDS`), also when it keeps the state: `require_base_branch` replacing the wait a renamed repository caused once the repository is back without its base branch;
- the event is a change the pipeline made without a human that the human must know about (`status_ledger.NOTICE_VERBS`): `follow_renamed_base` and `resume_renamed_base`, the record following a base branch the upstream renamed while the upstream PR is open; `verify_upstream_pr` when it names a base the upstream renamed before the PR was opened; `resume_upstream_read`, a wait that failed reads caused ending once the upstream is read again; and `resume_renamed_upstream`, the wait a renamed repository caused ending once GitHub answers for it under its recorded name again.

The wording of an entry is generated from the table (`status_ledger.table_message`), for every row: the verb, how it was taken (by a command, observation, the Result of requested Work, a remote operation, or the controller), the guards of the row that say why it applies (the exact revision, the stop flag, and the other guards every command row states are left out), the row's description, the state it leads to with the description of that state (or that the contribution stays in its state), the pending human action the row records, and the budgets it charges. What the event observed is added as bounded data after it: a head, the submission package or the upstream pull request the pipeline recorded, the human who requested the submission, a mismatch or an error (quoted as a code span or a block quote that cannot open a comment), the spent or exhausted counters, an admission override, and, for the nomination and a reminder at the gate, the exact `submit-upstream` command bound to the revision and head the event recorded. A comment an explanation row recorded is shown as a code span, so nothing in it renders. The entries about an unavailable upstream or a renamed base (`require_base_branch`, `exhaust_source_reads`, `require_upstream_access`, `require_renamed_upstream`, `follow_renamed_base`, `resume_renamed_base`, `resume_upstream_read`, `resume_renamed_upstream`, `verify_upstream_pr` when it names a renamed base, and the reminders of the waits for `base_branch_unavailable` and `upstream_unavailable`) are written in plain words instead (`status_ledger.plain_message`): what happened and why, what was observed, and what can be done (see [The upstream base branch](#the-upstream-base-branch)); they name no verb, budget, field, state, or pending action, and the commands below them are the only names a human types. An event recorded outside the table (intake, the record migration, a development PR receipt, `authorize-followup`) has fixed wording (`status_ledger.RECORDED_KINDS`), and an older kind is named as recorded.

An event that records neither the source nor the destination state in its `details` (an older event) is placed by folding the fields it changed over the events before it, and a state the table no longer has takes the kind of its phase when every state of that phase has the same kind. Every other event is shown only in the event log of the status page; the ledger reports it as `status_page_only` and never posts it, so it is neither retried nor a failure. A posted entry carries a hidden marker with the contribution and the event sequence, so an entry is posted once and a later tick repairs a failure after the state commit. Replies to commands are posted by `command_feedback` and `command_reactions`.

The [status page]({{ '/status/' | relative_url }}) is generated by `scripts/generate_doc.py` from committed state only. Under each contribution's row a disclosure, closed by default, opens its event log, newest event first. Each event shows its sequence, its time in UTC, its verb, its cause type, its source and destination state, and its revision, with links to what committed data resolves: the workflow run of the control repository that a cause names (a worker, planner, dispatcher, or controller run), commits on the fork, the development PR and the upstream PR, the tracking issue and the command comment, operations and their workflow runs, and evidence, with the CI run, its head, and its failed jobs for CI evidence. No free text of an event, evidence, or comment is shown; every value is escaped, and the same state gives the same page.

The status of each contribution comes from the table (`generate_doc.status_label`): its phase, and what the kind of its state says it waits for. A state of kind `gate`, `human_wait`, or `locked` waits for a human and names the pending human action. In a state of kind `auto_wait` the status names the first row the controller takes whose record guards hold, with the guards only the controller observes (a free worker slot, available capacity) as what it waits for, or else the first guard of such a row that does not hold; there is no separate capacity wait. A state of kind `work`, and an `auto_wait` state that only observation leaves, waits for the trigger of the rows that leave it: observation, the Result of requested Work, or a remote operation. A parked or closed contribution shows its phase, with the terminal reason, and a paused one is shown as paused with the status of its state.

## Reminders

Every tick, the controller takes `record_reminder` for each record in a state of kind `gate`, `human_wait`, or `locked` whose pending human step was requested, or last reminded, at least `human_sla_business_days.reminder` business days ago (Monday to Friday, counted in whole days from the exact time of the request or of the last reminder). The row sets `pending_human_action.reminded_at` and nothing else; the revision is kept. While the record is paused no reminder is posted. The event carries the pending action and, for `handoff.awaiting_submission`, the revision and candidate head; its tracking-issue entry repeats the exact `submit-upstream` command for them. In `handoff.waiting_upstream_pr` the event also carries the link to the posted submission package, the human who requested the submission, and what the observation of the same tick found that does not match: the entry links the package, names the exact clean head, mentions that human, and states the mismatch when there is one; its possible next commands carry the exact `retract-pr` command.

## Requesting upstream submission

`handoff.awaiting_submission` is the gate at which a human requests upstream submission. The tracking-issue entry for `nominate_for_handoff` names the candidate head on which CI passed and shows the command to post:

```text
/upstreaming submit-upstream revision=N head=<full head SHA>
```

The grammar requires the revision and the full 40-character head. The engine accepts the command exactly as the table defines `submit_upstream`: the revision is the current one, the head is the candidate head recorded in `handoff_request` and still the head of `riscv64-dev`, and the contribution is not paused. A resume from a pause bumps the revision and keeps the candidate head, so the command then names the new revision and the same head. Outside the handoff phase the command is rejected with a reason that names the state the contribution is in and `handoff.awaiting_submission`, the state the command applies in.

`submit_upstream` leads to `handoff.review_due` and authorizes the submission rewrite, its review, and the deterministic handoff preparation described in [The submission rewrite and review](#the-submission-rewrite-and-review) and [The handoff operations](#the-handoff-operations). It records `handoff_approval` (the command, its actor, the exact revision and head, and the CI and plan evidence it saw); the rewrite Work is allocated by the controller in the same tick when a worker slot is free. No second command is needed until the upstream PR is open. `riscv64-dev` and the development PR are never changed for the submission, and the development PR is never closed.

The commands `request-handoff`, `approve-handoff`, and `register-upstream-pr` are retired. Each is rejected with a message that names `submit-upstream`.

`/upstreaming retract-pr revision=N head=<clean head>` withdraws the submission package in `handoff.waiting_upstream_pr` (`retract_submission`, to `development.fix_needed`, with the exact revision, the exact clean head of `riscv64`, and quiescence). The row reads no reason, so the command takes none. It bumps the revision, clears the pending action, `handoff_request`, and `handoff_approval`, so reminders stop, and unlocks branch work. No upstream resource is changed, and `riscv64` is left as it is: the next `submit_upstream` replaces it.

## The submission rewrite and review

Two uncredentialed Work tasks prepare the submission, one after the other, each in a fresh context, and each charged to `work_total` like every Work. Both run in the worker workflow, with the verified plan and its contribution guidelines, the approved head of `riscv64-dev`, the frozen base, the plan's measurable commit rules (`context.commit_rules`), and the required sections of the pull request template (`context.template_sections`).

In `handoff.review_due` the controller allocates the rewrite (`request_submission_rewrite`, task `submission_rewrite`). The model reshapes the non-debug commits of the approved series on the base after the project's commit conventions (squashing only where the contributing instructions or the reviewers require it, with the same final tree, and keeping every commit's tree a tree of the approved series without its debug commits, in its order), and writes the upstream PR title and description after the pull request template, every section filled and every checklist item answered honestly, and after the contributing instructions, which take precedence over the pipeline's instructions for content and style but never widen the trusted gates, the scope, or the credentials. It returns a rewrite (`schemas/submission_rewrite.schema.json`: verdict, reasons, title, description, and the digest of the series) and the reshaped series as a `git format-patch` mbox. A rewrite that finds an expectation the series cannot meet without a code change fails with its reasons.

The worker's trusted publish job checks the rewrite without any token (`submission.check_rewrite`), before any review: the rewrite answers the Work, the series matches its digest, has at most 50 commits (counted in the mbox before anything is rebuilt and in the rebuilt series before any commit is validated), rebuilds on the base under the App identity to a deterministic head, and passes the publisher's validation of an upstream-facing rewrite (admitted scope, no debug commit, no merge, no forbidden workflow content, no symlink, gitlink, or new executable mode); so does every commit of the series, not only its head: each commit is validated against the base like the head, each path a commit touches is a path the approved series without its debug commits changes, every file of every reviewed tree is the base's or has the path, mode, and blob it has in a tree of the approved series without its debug commits, and every reviewed tree is the tree of a commit of that series later than the tree of the reviewed commit before it, so commits may be squashed and reworded but a series that reorders them, splits them into other trees, or adds and later reverts anything, including content the approved series removed, is refused; every attribution trailer (`Signed-off-by:`, `Co-authored-by:`, `Reviewed-by:`, and every other `*-by:` line, the title included, recognized after NFKC normalization and case folding with every hyphen-like character read as `-` and every colon-like character as `:`) names the App's commit identity or is byte-identical to a trailer of the approved commits, and no attribution trailer is followed by a line that starts with white space, which `git interpret-trailers` would fold into it; its final tree is exactly the tree of the approved series without its `{debug}` commits; every commit follows the measurable commit rules, including the number of commits; and the description has every required section of the template as a Markdown heading and at most 12000 characters. A plan of schema version 1 records no rules and no sections: the rules and sections the rewrite reports having found in the project are applied instead, which can only refuse.

The reconciler folds the trusted outcome. A pass takes `accept_submission_rewrite` to `handoff.review_pending` and records the rewrite evidence (`submission_rewrite_id`: the title and description and their digest, every commit message and tree, the rewritten head, the clean tree, the tree of the candidate head, and the worker run and publication artifact that hold the series); the revision is bumped, since the review is new Work. A failed rewrite or a failed check takes `reject_submission_rewrite` to `development.fix_needed`: the revision is bumped, the reasons are recorded as rewrite evidence and become pending feedback that the next development Work carries, and nothing is posted or written.

In `handoff.review_pending` the controller allocates the review (`request_submission_review`, task `submission_review`). Its payload names the accepted rewrite (`context.rewrite`: the evidence, the run and artifact, and the digests of the series and the text). Before the model runs, the trusted pre-agent step downloads the rewrite's `porting-publication` artifact with the job's own read token, checks its id, name, and run, and checks the series and the title and description against their digests; the model reads exactly those. It reviews the series and the text against the pull request template, the contributing instructions, the commit conventions, and the reviewers' expectations, and returns pass or fail with one reason per problem and each expectation with whether it is met (`schemas/submission_review.schema.json`). It returns no series and changes nothing. The publish job checks it without a token (`submission.check_review`): it answers the Work and names the digests of exactly that series and text, and a pass lists no unmet expectation. A pass takes `accept_submission_review` to `handoff.building_upstream_branch`; the review evidence (`submission_review_id`) carries the rewrite it passed, which the build of `riscv64` and the package read. A fail takes `reject_submission_review` to `development.fix_needed` with its reasons as pending feedback.

Both Work also return the worker's report. The check refuses an outcome whose Work carries a history and whose report is missing or invalid, as an unusable outcome; the report is stored as `worker_report` evidence only by `accept_submission_rewrite` and `accept_submission_review`. The review reads the reports of the earlier workers in its history to know why the series has its shape.

An outcome of either Work that is not usable (the Result could not be verified, the check could not run, or the Result is malformed, answers other Work, or names another series or text) is Work without a usable Result, never a verdict: `allocate_work_retry` within `work_recovery` and `work_total`, then `exhaust_work_recovery` to `handoff.waiting_human`, whose `resume_submission_review` returns to `handoff.review_due` for a fresh rewrite. A pause in `handoff.agent_running` or `handoff.reviewing` leads to `handoff.review_due`.

The rewritten series is held in the rewrite run's publication artifact, which GitHub keeps 30 days, or the number of days the repository variable `GH_AW_DEFAULT_ARTIFACT_RETENTION_DAYS` sets (the generated worker lock uses it, and the controller's workflow hands it to the tick). When it has expired or expires within a day, `expire_submission_rewrite` leads back to `handoff.review_due` for a fresh rewrite: in `handoff.review_pending` before the review is allocated, and in `handoff.building_upstream_branch` before an attempt is planned, also after a long human wait that resumed to the build.

## The handoff operations

Two handoff states each run one remote operation through the trusted workflow `handoff.yml`, which also runs the `ci_rerun` operation of `handoff.waiting_ci` (its `ci-rerun` job, see [CI reruns](#ci-reruns)). No model runs there and no credential can write upstream; the authorized human opens the upstream PR under their own account.

| State | Operation kind | Effect and readback | Token |
|:------|:---------------|:--------------------|:------|
| `handoff.building_upstream_branch` | `upstream_branch` | The reviewed series is downloaded from the `porting-publication` artifact of the worker run the accepted review names (the artifact's id, name, and run are checked, and the series must match the review's digest), rebuilt on the frozen base in a fresh repository without any credential (the public fork and upstream are fetched anonymously), and checked again, all before any token is minted: the reviewed head, the clean tree, the commit messages, and every check of the submission check. After the mint only the series digest and the head and tree of the prepared repository are verified again; the write token only pushes and never fetches. `riscv64` is then created at that head (`--force-with-lease=refs/heads/riscv64:`, which requires that it does not exist) or replaced (`--force-with-lease=refs/heads/riscv64:<observed head>`), and read back. Nothing is written while a pull request is open from `riscv64` (upstream or in the fork), while `riscv64-dev` is missing, or while the record does not develop on `riscv64-dev`, and nothing is written when `riscv64` already resolves to the reviewed head. `riscv64-dev` is only read. When the clean head has another tree than the candidate head, the same attempt dispatches the project's required CI workflow on `refs/heads/riscv64` if it can be dispatched, and reads its run back by head. | destination App, the one fork, `contents: write`, `workflows: write`; a second token of the destination App on the one fork with `actions: write` only (the CI dispatch), minted only for an attempt whose clean head has another tree than the candidate head; the job's own token reads the control repository's artifact (`actions: read`) |
| `handoff.preparing_submission` | `submission_comment` | One App-authored comment on the tracking issue carries the submission package, found again by its hidden marker (contribution, approved revision, clean head) so it is posted once; it is read back. | control App, `porting-agent`, `issues: write` |

Each job of the workflow checks out the state at the head of `main` when it starts, not the commit the run was dispatched at, and before it mints its token checks that this commit is still the head of `main` (`handoff.current_state`) and that the state still runs the operation, of the job's kind (`python -m handoff check`); a refusal there is the job's receipt and its later steps do not run, so a queued or re-run job never acts on state that `main` has left. Before any effect the workflow checks the operation against that state again (`handoff.load_request`): the operation is `planned` and of the kind of the record's state, the record names it as its active operation and is not paused, the policy permits the write, and the target is the recorded fork and `riscv64` with the approved head and the accepted review whose head, tree, and series digest the operation names, or the tracking issue at the recorded clean head. A refused check writes nothing and its receipt is not folded. Immediately before each write (the push of `riscv64`, the CI dispatch, the post of the package, the rerun request of the `ci-rerun` job) the workflow reads the head of `main` again (`handoff.unchanged_on_main`): the same commit as the checkout passes; otherwise `main` must contain the checkout, and none of the policy, the record, the evidence it names (plan, CI, rewrite, review), or the operation (other than its launch count) may have changed, read from the comparison of the two commits. An unrelated commit (another contribution, an event) does not refuse, and neither does a change to code or workflows on `main`: pause and `read_only` are the operator's stops. Anything else, an unreadable or truncated comparison included, refuses without the write, as a `refused` receipt (no receipt for the rerun) that is not folded and is launched again within `operation_launch`; a refusal after the push of `riscv64` and before the CI dispatch leaves `riscv64` at the reviewed head, so the next launch writes nothing and only dispatches. A change committed after that read is not seen: an effect that passed it may finish, as the table says of a pause, and the reconciler still reads every effect back. Every other failure of an attempt is reported by a receipt: `invalid` for a reviewed series that does not rebuild to the reviewed head or a lease on `riscv64` that conflicts (the branch moved), `blocked` for an upstream PR open from `riscv64`, `infrastructure` for everything else.

The controller records the intent of each attempt and launches it like the other workflow operations: counted on the operation (`operation_launch`), again after `LAUNCH_INTERVAL_SECONDS` without a receipt, and not while the contribution is paused. A launched attempt is also held while a run of it is in flight: the controller workflow reads the `handoff.yml` runs that are not completed before it collects the receipts (`reconcile --handoff-runs`) and matches them to operations by their run name `Handoff <operation id>`, which is only compared with the operation id pattern, never rendered. While such a run is listed, or while the run list could not be read in the tick, the attempt is neither launched again nor closed, for at most `handoff.RUN_SECONDS` (3600 seconds, the longest job chain of `handoff.yml` behind the longest run of the concurrency group `porting-fork-writer`, rounded up) after the last launch; the tick report lists it under `deferred`. Each attempt is its own operation of the same intent: its reason ids are the approval, `attempt:<n>`, and `count:<k>`, the count of the budget of the state (`upstream_branch`, `submission_comment`) at that attempt, and for the build the accepted review. The reconciler folds a receipt only for the open operation the record runs, reads back what it reports (`riscv64` at the reviewed head and tree, and the series from the base to it with exactly the reviewed commit messages; the App-authored comment with its marker and digest), and fires the row bound in `recovery.FIRES`:

- a confirmed attempt fires `confirm_clean_head` (sets `clean_head_sha` to the head of `riscv64` and starts the rerun counts) or `confirm_submission_package` (sets the pending action `open_upstream_pr` and `handoff_request`, whose `command_id` is the package operation);
- a failed attempt fires the retry row while the count is below the limit (`retries.infrastructure_per_head_failure` plus one attempts), and the next attempt is planned and launched in the same tick; at the limit it fires the exhaustion row to `handoff.waiting_human` with the pending action and the counter of the budget, and the next attempt counts from one after the resume;
- an `invalid` build fires `return_for_repair` to `development.fix_needed`, and a `blocked` one `require_branch_intervention` to `handoff.waiting_human` with the pending action `upstream_branch_blocked`, whose `resume_upstream_branch` builds `riscv64` again;
- a `moved` package attempt, read back by the reconciler, fires `return_for_repair` to `development.fix_needed`; one the reconciler does not read back as moved is retried. A `moved` class on any other kind is an infrastructure failure.

An attempt whose launches are spent without a receipt (a cancelled or timed-out run, a lost runner, a dispatch that failed) is a definite infrastructure failure: once the last allowed launch is `LAUNCH_INTERVAL_SECONDS` old and no run of the attempt is in flight, or once it is `handoff.RUN_SECONDS` old whatever the run list says, and the contribution is not paused, the controller closes it as failed (`begin_effect`, `record_failure`, with `failure` `infrastructure` and `launches_spent`) and fires the same retry or exhaustion row as a failed receipt (`handoff.fail_attempt`); a retry plans and launches the next attempt in the same tick. Every handoff work state therefore ends in its next state or in `handoff.waiting_human`, whose resume starts the budget again. A receipt of the closed attempt that arrives later is not folded. A slow or queued healthy run is therefore not charged: its attempt stays planned until its receipt is folded normally.

Git receives the fork token as configuration in the environment of the git process (`GIT_CONFIG_COUNT`), never on its command line; the publisher does the same.

`handoff.waiting_ci` is observed like development CI, on the clean head of `riscv64`, with jobs that lost their runner classified: `accept_clean_head_ci` when every required job executed and passed (kept while an operation of the contribution is open), `return_for_repair` for a blocking failure, which records the failing evidence as `last_ci_evidence_id` (annotated with the candidate head when the clean head is another commit, so the repair names the failing job and charges its cluster), or for a `riscv64` head that is not the clean head, which keeps the recorded evidence, and `rerun_interrupted_ci` or `exhaust_interrupted_reruns` for a required job that was cancelled or lost its runner while no job failed on its own (see [CI reruns](#ci-reruns)). The `ci-rerun` job of `handoff.yml` holds one token, of the destination App, for the one fork, with `actions: write` only, minted after its check. CI is a function of the tree: when the reviewed series has the tree of the candidate head (the approved series had no debug commit), the CI evidence of the candidate head on `riscv64-dev` is read for it. A run still in progress, a missing run, or interrupted CI that no row could rerun keeps the wait, within bounds: `riscv64` has no pull request, so when the clean head has another tree, the build of `riscv64` reads the workflow of the required CI run on the candidate head (`workflow_path` of its evidence) at the clean head, as data, and when its triggers include `workflow_dispatch` with no required input it dispatches it on `riscv64` and reads its run back by the head SHA (`external_ids.ci_dispatch` of the operation records `dispatched`, `exists`, `unsupported`, or `not_needed`). The wait starts when the clean head is confirmed (`clean_head_ci_since`). `require_clean_head_ci` leads to `handoff.waiting_human` with the pending action `clean_head_ci_missing` at once when no run exists and the workflow cannot be dispatched, and in any case once CI on the clean head is not complete 12 hours after the wait began (`lifecycle.CLEAN_HEAD_CI_WAIT_HOURS`). Reminders apply. `resume_clean_head_ci` returns to `handoff.waiting_ci`, observes CI on the clean head again, and starts the bounded wait again; `rerun_interrupted_ci` and `resume_ci` start it again too.

`handoff.waiting_upstream_pr` is locked: its exits are `verify_upstream_pr`, `retract_submission`, and `refresh_planning` (with the exact clean head), and nothing else changes its state. The observation lists the open pull requests of the upstream repository whose head is `riscv64` of the fork owner and verifies exactly one that is open, against the upstream repository and the frozen base, from the fork's `riscv64` at the exact clean head, and authored by a human whose account id is in `command_authority.actor_ids`. It records `upstream_pr` with that author and enters `upstream_review.waiting_ci`. A `riscv64` head that is not the clean head, a candidate that fails a filter, or several matching candidates take `record_audit_observation` once per distinct observation (its key is in the event), and the next reminder states it. No Work, no publication, no write to the fork, and no automatic exit happens in the wait.

### The submission package

The package is derived from the committed record, the verified plan, the accepted submission review, the CI evidence of the clean head, and the commits from the base to the clean head of `riscv64`, which must be the reviewed series (`handoff.build_package`), so the same inputs give the same bytes:

- the title is the reviewed title, at most 120 characters;
- the description is the reviewed description, written after the project's pull request template and contributing instructions; at most 12000 characters;
- the prefilled link is `https://github.com/<upstream>/compare/<base>...<fork owner>:riscv64?quick_pull=1&title=<encoded>&body=<encoded>`, every value percent-encoded; when it would be longer than 8000 characters it carries the title only and the comment says to paste the description;
- in the title and the description an HTML entity of an at sign (`&#64;`, `&#x40;`, `&commat;`, and those of the fullwidth and small at signs) is decoded, then every at sign that is followed by any character other than white space (a character reference such as `&#117;` can decode to a login character) and not preceded by an ASCII letter or digit gets a word joiner (U+2060) after it, whatever the Markdown around it (code, emphasis, escapes, unclosed fences), so the upstream pull request notifies nobody; an e-mail address keeps its at sign. The comment says so whenever anything was changed;
- the comment shows the upstream repository and base with its id, the source fork and branch `riscv64` with its id, the approved head of `riscv64-dev` it was built from and the development PR, which stays open, the exact head, the changed files (cut with the count of the files it leaves out when the whole list would not fit the comment), the number of required CI jobs that passed and the head they ran on, the package operation, the approval command, the plan evidence, and the review evidence, a warning to check that the compare form shows exactly that head (with the exact `retract-pr` command otherwise, and a note that after a pause and its resume the latest tracking-issue entry carries the current revision), the title and the full description outside the link, the contribution rules of the plan, that the pull request is opened at exactly that head with no commit added to `riscv64` and no file edited in the form, that a change the contribution policy needs in the series (for example a CHANGELOG entry or a commit sign-off) withdraws the package with the `retract-pr` command for a repair in development instead of being submitted, and that only steps outside the series (such as signing a CLA) are the human's before submitting, the admitted paths the series does not change as a reason to withdraw, that the title and description follow the template and should be reviewed before submitting, what to do when the human's account cannot open the pull request (another human of the command authority opens it from the same package, never from another fork or branch), and the hidden marker;
- every untrusted value (the contribution rules, the admitted paths, the changed files, the commit messages, the CI job names, the reviewed title and description) is in a code span with its backticks replaced or in a fenced block longer than any backtick run it contains, so none of it renders as a mention, a link, an image, or HTML; the only mention is the human who requested the submission. Untrusted text is also stripped of credential patterns and of HTML comment openers and closers, removed until none is left, so no removal joins the rest into a new one.

The workflow posts the package only while `riscv64` still resolves to the clean head; a branch at another head, or one missing (deleted or renamed) from the fork read under its recorded repository id, fails the attempt with the class `moved`, and a read that establishes neither is an infrastructure failure. The reconciler reads `riscv64` again (`handoff.moved_readback`): when it is still off the clean head, `return_for_repair` returns the contribution to `development.fix_needed`, keeps the recorded CI evidence (a moved branch has no CI verdict), and charges no `submission_comment` budget; when it is read at the clean head or cannot be read, the failure is folded as an infrastructure failure (`retry_submission_comment`, then `exhaust_comment_retries`). A package already posted is found by its marker and read back: only an App-authored comment whose bytes are this package, or whose last line is the marker and which names the approval command in its package operation line, counts, so a comment of anyone else or one that only carries a marker is never taken for the package.

## Record schema and its migration

The record schema (`schemas/contribution.schema.json`) is strict: every field of the record is required, as are the counts of `budget` (`reruns` among them) except `source_unavailable`, `source_unavailable_since`, and `review_rejections` (the count of `dev_reviews`), which an absent count reads as zero and no migration adds, the counter of a pending human action, and the fields of the Work identity in `active_work` (`generation`, `attempt`, `dispatched_run_id`, `reason_ids`, and `feedback_evidence_ids`, which the allocation rows set, empty for Work that carries no feedback). The stop flag is `paused` alone: the record has no `blocked_reason`. The enums of `phase` and `activity` are the phases and activities of the states of the table, and the counter a pending human action names is a budget of the table; `scripts/generate_state_machine_doc.py` writes them into the schema, and its `--check` fails when the committed schema differs. A record is in a state of the table, and a human wait of the handoff phase carries a pending action the table enters it with.

A record written before the schema was strict can lack fields the table added later and can still carry `blocked_reason`, always null. The controller rewrites such a record itself, before it does anything else in a tick (`state.migrate_record_fields`):

- Each missing field is added with the value the pipeline read for an absent one: null, an empty list, false, or zero, as `engine.RECORD_DEFAULTS`, `engine.BUDGET_DEFAULTS`, and `engine.WORK_DEFAULTS` list them. `budget.reruns` starts for the recorded head (zero reruns), as the engine counted absent rerun counts. A missing `pending_human_action.counter` is null. The null `blocked_reason` is removed, and so is `budget.reruns.answered_ci_evidence_id`, whatever its value: the fence of the CI run a `request_ci_rerun` answered before the trusted rerun, which the confirmed rerun operation replaces.
- Nothing else changes: the state, the revision, the generation, the fork, the development PR, the head, the evidence, the budgets that are recorded, and the replay fences are kept. The review of development patches added `held_patch` and `review_comments`, which every record written before it gains as null and empty this way, with its `record_fields_migrated` event: Work requested before it (with no review contract in its payload) keeps it and still publishes its Result in `development.agent_running`.
- A record with a set `blocked_reason`, a state outside the table, or any other difference from the strict schema has no migration: the controller stops with an error and writes nothing.
- The record and one appended event of kind `record_fields_migrated` (cause `migration`, `strict-record-schema`, with the added and removed fields in its details) are the only paths of that state commit; the tick requests no effect. The event is shown in the event log of the status page only. The state publisher's final check accepts the commit and refuses any commit that writes a record without the fields again. The migration is idempotent: a migrated record is not selected again.

The planning phase and its plan were once named `investigation` and `dossier`; `lifecycle.HISTORIC_NAMES` maps each name that is no longer written (the phase and its states, the verbs of its rows, the budget `investigation_attempts`, the pending action `investigation_failed`, the evidence kind `dossier`, and the field `dossier_evidence_id`) to the name that replaced it. The controller rewrites state written under the historic names in the same migration-only commit, before the field migration (`state.migrate_record_names`, with `engine.with_current_names` for a record):

- A record: the fields `dossier_evidence_id` and `budget.investigation_attempts` take their current names with their values, and the historic values of `phase`, `previous_phase`, `pending_human_action.kind`, and `pending_human_action.counter` their current values. A project: the field `dossier_evidence_id`. An evidence record: the kind `dossier` becomes `plan`; its id is kept, since operations and events name it.
- Nothing else changes, `updated_at` included. Requested Work of the historic task `investigation` has no migration (its queued payload names a worker profile that no longer exists), and neither has a record that carries a field under both names: the controller stops with an error and writes nothing.
- One appended event of kind `record_names_migrated` per contribution (cause `migration`, `plan-rename`, with the renamed places, the renamed evidence of the contribution, and the renamed projects of which it is the first contribution in its details) records it. Until the controller runs, `python -m state validate` reads each such file as the migration rewrites it and names it as awaiting the migration; the state publisher refuses any commit that writes a historic name again.
- Committed events are never rewritten: they are the history as it was written, and the event schema accepts any kind. The tracking-issue ledger reads a historic verb, state, or phase of an event under its current name (`lifecycle.current_name`), and the event log of the status page shows an event as it was recorded. A submission package posted before the rename names its plan evidence `dossier evidence`, and its discovery accepts both.

Records written before the lifecycle table (the old handoff approval wait, the `blocked` stop flag, the terminal phases `abandoned`, `no_work`, `deferred`, and `already_released`, and the provisioning and development states the table replaced) were rewritten by earlier migrations, whose events stay in the event log. A record in one of those shapes is not valid state any more: the controller stops with an error and writes nothing.

## Remote operations

Every remote effect is an operation (`.state/operations/`), whose states and transitions are the operation table, `lifecycle.OPERATIONS`. `recovery.py` is its engine and the one write path of an operation's `status`:

- `recovery.plan` takes the initial verb `record_intent`: the operation is `planned`, before any effect.
- `recovery.apply_operation(op, verb, ...)` takes one row from the operation's status: every guard is a named predicate in `recovery.PREDICATES`, either a fact the caller establishes or a check of the command (`resolve-operation` names this operation, the resolution evidence record names this operation and states the disposition the command states, the disposition). The row's effects are applied exactly: `external_ids` merged, one `discovery` entry appended, `resolution` recorded from the command, its actor, and the evidence. A refused row changes nothing; a row the table does not have raises.
- The caller names its observation with the helpers: `mark_applying` (`begin_effect`: capacity available, the contribution not paused, the final check before the effect passed), `mark_result` (`record_response` for a success or a lost response, `record_failure` for a definite failure), `record_discovery` (`confirm_by_discovery` for the exact resource, `record_discovery` for an absent or unrelated one; only an `uncertain` operation is discovered), `fold_receipt`, and `resolve`.

Who takes which rows:

| Operation | Rows | Taken by |
|:----------|:-----|:---------|

A planned operation that a trusted workflow runs stays `planned` across its launches: each launch is counted on the operation in `external_ids.launch_attempts`, the counter of `operation_launch`, and a launch is repeated only after `LAUNCH_INTERVAL_SECONDS` (300) without a receipt. For a record in `provisioning.idle` the launch is the lifecycle row `start_operation`: the controller records the intent and takes the row in the same tick, the row charges `operation_launch`, and the controller counts the launch on the operation. Once the budget is exhausted (`retries.infrastructure_per_head_failure` plus one launches) and the last launch is older than the interval, the controller takes `require_provisioning_intervention` to `provisioning.waiting_human` with the pending action `provisioning_launch_exhausted` and the counter `operation_launch`; nothing is launched while the contribution is paused. A resume that starts `operation_launch` again sets the launches of the planned operation back to zero. When the intent of `provisioning.idle` derives the id of an operation that is already closed (a human resolved the planned operation `not-launched` or `confirmed` in `provisioning.idle`, which fires no row and releases it, also while paused), the controller plans the intent again as a new operation, adding `replan:<n>` to its reason ids, and launches it in the same tick within its own `operation_launch`; a re-plan after `confirmed` finds the fork already in place, and its receipt and readback take `confirm_provisioning`. Once `MAX_REPLANS` (20) re-plans of the intent at one revision are closed, the controller takes `require_provisioning_intervention` to `provisioning.waiting_human` with the pending action `provisioning_launch_exhausted`, the counter `operation_launch` and `replans` in its details, whose reminders apply as in every wait and whose resume plans at a new revision. For a record in `development.waiting_ci` the launch of a development PR operation is the row `launch_development_pr`: the controller plans a `dev_pr_create` operation when the published head has no recorded development PR, and a `dev_pr_update` operation (the CI bootstrap) when the PR was created at this head and no CI evidence is recorded, records the intent and takes the row in the same tick, and takes it again for each launch after the interval without a verified receipt. A launched development PR operation is also held while a run of it is in flight: the controller workflow reads the `dev-pr.yml` runs that are not completed before it collects the receipts (`reconcile --development-pr-runs`) and matches them by their run name `Development PR <operation id>`, which is only compared with the operation id pattern, never rendered. While such a run is listed, or the run list could not be read, the operation is neither launched again nor exhausted, for at most `DEV_PR_RUN_SECONDS` (1800 seconds: `dev-pr.yml` shares the concurrency group `porting-fork-writer` with the handoff workflow) after the last launch. Once `operation_launch` is exhausted and the last launch is older than the interval, the controller takes `exhaust_development_pr_launches` to `development.waiting_human` with the pending action `development_pr_launch_exhausted` and the counter `operation_launch`, and closes the operation as failed and releases it, so `park`, `refresh` and the stale resume apply in the wait. Its entry and reminders name the operation and ask the human to read the `Development PR <operation id>` runs of the control repository, correct the cause, and resume; `resume_development_pr` returns to `development.waiting_ci` at a new revision, where a new operation is planned with a new id (a re-plan of a closed intent at the same revision adds `replan:<n>` to its reason ids). A planned development PR operation of a paused contribution, or one that no longer matches the record (another state, revision, fork, head or base, or a PR already recorded for a create, or none for the CI bootstrap), is closed as failed and released without a row; nothing is launched while the contribution is paused. The workflow creates or adopts the same-fork PR `riscv64-dev` -> base: an open PR that carries this contribution's marker (`<!-- upstreaming-development:<contribution>:`), of any revision or head, is adopted, so a PR a lost launch left is never duplicated; any other open PR from `riscv64-dev`, or two marked ones, fails the launch. The launches of a `ci_rerun` operation are counted the same way, within the same limit, without a row of the table. The launches of a handoff attempt are counted the same way, and additionally wait while a run of the attempt is in flight, for at most `handoff.RUN_SECONDS` after the last launch.

The provisioning workflow (`provision.yml`, `python -m provisioning`) sorts a run that does not provision into three classes. A definite failure of the effect uploads a receipt with status `failed`, the step (`preflight`, `create` or `initialize`) and a closed failure code (`provisioning.FAILURES`): `source_changed` (the upstream repository or base commit changed identity), `fork_mismatch` (the fork is not the approved upstream's fork, changed id, or disappeared), `branch_conflict` (`riscv64-dev` holds work the pipeline did not record, is gone, or does not read back), `settings` (the Actions settings do not read back, or the workflows stay unregistered), `workflows` (the inherited workflows cannot be inventoried or read within the bounded scan), `write_refused`, or `other`. A write is refused definitely when GitHub answers 403, 404 or 422, unless a 403 carries `retry-after` or `x-ratelimit-remaining: 0` (rate limiting). The reconciler folds it with `record_failure`, stores the code on the operation as `external_ids.provision_failure`, and from `provisioning.idle` takes `require_provisioning_intervention` with `provisioning_operation_failed` and no counter, at once: a definite failure is not retried. A refusal before any effect (the policy is read-only, the contribution is paused or no longer in provisioning, the operation is no longer the record's current planned intent, or the preconditions changed between steps) uploads a receipt with status `refused`, which the reconciler reports and does not fold: the final check before the effect did not pass, so `begin_effect` cannot be taken. Any other failure (a failed read, a write answered with anything but a definite refusal, a lost response, a token that could not be minted, a lost runner, a timeout) uploads no receipt, and the operation is launched again within `operation_launch`; provisioning adopts the fork and never resets the branch, so the launch again is the bounded retry. A failing step fails the job, so no later token is minted. The controller collects provisioning receipts only from `provision.yml` runs on `main` it dispatched (`branch=main&event=workflow_dispatch`), since a failure receipt has nothing to read back. Of several foldable receipts (`provisioned` or `failed`) for one operation, only the newest run's is folded, and the older ones are reported `superseded`. The receipt's free-text reason is cleaned, bounded, and kept in the tick report only, never in the record, the event, or the tracking issue: the tracking issue entry and its reminders name the operation and one fixed sentence for the failure code, and ask the human to read the `Fork Provisioning <operation id>` run, correct the cause, and resume or park.

`resume_provisioning` requires that the prerequisite is corrected. The controller establishes that fact for `/upstreaming resume` in `provisioning.waiting_human` when the pending action is one it wrote: `provisioning_launch_exhausted` with the counter `operation_launch`, or `provisioning_operation_failed`. The human's resume is the statement that the cause is corrected. After exhausted launches the planned operation is still open: the resume bumps the revision, starts its launches again from zero, and the same operation is launched again from `provisioning.idle`. The provisioning workflow accepts an operation of an earlier revision when the record names it as its active operation. After a failed operation the record names no operation: the resume returns to `provisioning.idle`, where a new operation is planned at the new revision, with a new id. A provisioning receipt that arrives while the record waits closes the operation and keeps the wait, so the resume also plans a new operation then. The wait after exhausted launches keeps its planned operation open and so keeps the fence: once the wait is older than `refresh.stale_after_days`, `resume_provisioning` no longer applies, and `park` and `resume_stale` require no open operation. The exit is then: a human who verified what the `Fork Provisioning <operation id>` runs did closes the operation with `/upstreaming resolve-operation operation=<id> disposition=not-launched|failed reason="..."`, which only closes and releases it in the wait, then posts `park` (to `parked.idle`) or `resume` (`resume_stale`, to `queued.idle`). The same holds while paused: `resolve-operation` is admitted by the mask of the flag, and the `resume` after it then takes `resume_stale`. The entry and reminders of the wait, the "Possible next commands" of a wait or pause with an open operation, and the reply to a `resume`, `park` or `refresh` the fence refuses name that command with the pipeline's own operation id.

An operation id is derived from the contribution, the revision, the kind, and the reason ids (`recovery.operation_id`), so the same intent yields the same id. An id is never used again once its operation closed: a `ci_rerun` operation of interrupted CI names the count of `interrupted_reruns` before its charge, and one of the publication job names the evidence, the Work, and the publish attempt; a publish-only retry of the same Result adds `publish-attempt:<attempt>` to the reason ids from the second attempt on (the attempt the retry was dispatched with), and a CI bootstrap `dev_pr_update` planned again after a failed or never-launched one adds `replan:<n>`, the number of earlier ones for the same head. The first attempt and the first plan keep the id they always had.

An operation that closes fires the lifecycle row bound to it in `recovery.FIRES`, by the state of the contribution and the end state of the operation, through `engine.apply_transition` (trigger `operation`): a confirmed `fork_provision` operation in `provisioning.idle` takes `confirm_provisioning` to `development.fix_needed` with the fork, without a revision bump; a failed one takes `require_provisioning_intervention` with the pending action `provisioning_operation_failed` and no counter. A confirmed `dev_pr_create` or `dev_pr_update` operation in `development.waiting_ci` takes `confirm_development_pr`, which records `fork.pr_number` without a revision bump, also while paused; the reconciler requires the receipt and the exact readback of the open PR (its id, head, head repository, base, and this contribution's marker). A verified development PR receipt that arrives in another development state closes the operation and keeps the state, without recording the PR, which the next create adopts. A failed development PR operation fires no row, and a human `resolve-operation` of one only closes and releases it. The handoff operations fire their confirm, retry, and exhaustion rows, and `return_for_repair` for an invalid build and for a package attempt that finds `riscv64` moved. When several rows are bound, the one whose guards hold is taken: the caller supplies the count of the budget for a failed handoff attempt, and none for an invalid build or a moved branch, so exactly one applies. `active_operation_id` names an open operation only: the record's reference is released in the same write when the operation closes, also when no row is bound (a receipt that arrives in a human wait closes the operation and keeps the state).

`/upstreaming resolve-operation operation=<id> disposition=confirmed|failed|not-launched reason="..."` is accepted from an authorized command actor (checked at intake), only on the tracking issue of the operation's contribution, and takes the one `resolve_*` row whose guards hold. The controller is the producer of its evidence: from the authenticated command and its own snapshot of the operation it writes a record of kind `resolution` (`controller.resolution_evidence`) with the id `resolution-<operation id>-<comment id>` and the fields `resolution.operation_id`, `disposition`, `command_id`, `comment_id`, `issue_number`, `content_digest` (of the comment body), `actor`, `reason`, `operation` (its kind, status before the row, revision, launch count, number of discoveries, and last update), `contribution_state` and `paused`. The pipeline does not verify the human's statement; the record makes it auditable. The reason is untrusted text: it is bounded at intake to 2000 characters as stored (each U+2014 is stored as two hyphens, so the parser counts it as two; a longer reason rejects the command, for every command with a `reason`), and it is stored as data in the evidence only, is never in the summary, and is never rendered. The evidence is staged only when the row is taken, with the operation, the record and the receipt of the consumed comment, so they land in one state commit: there is never evidence without a resolution or a resolution without evidence, and `state validate` checks that every resolved operation names an existing resolution record of that operation. A command in the old form, with `evidence=<id>`, is rejected with a reply that names the new form. A resolution fires the bound lifecycle row only when it establishes the row's guards: a failed provisioning operation takes `require_provisioning_intervention` with the pending action `provisioning_operation_failed` and no counter; `confirm_provisioning` needs the receipt and the readback, so a provisioning operation resolved `confirmed` only closes the operation. A resolution that fires no row stages no event: the receipt of the command and the evidence record are its trail. The reply to a `resume`, `park` or `refresh` that the fence of an open operation refuses names the resolution only in a wait or a pause; elsewhere the operation is in flight and the reply names it only. A resolution of an operation whose contribution has no record is rejected as belonging to no tracked contribution.

An open operation (`planned`, `applying`, or `uncertain`) fences its contribution exactly as the guards of the table say: `park`, `refresh`, the stale resume, `restart_generation`, and the resumes guarded by quiescence are refused with the open operation named, planning Work is not dispatched, and the publisher's final check refuses a fork write while the record names an active operation.

The state publisher's final check refuses a commit that changes an operation's status along no sequence of rows (a closed operation never changes status). Before that, `reconcile.tick` checks every operation the tick stages against the committed one with the same check (`recovery.transition_problem`). A staged operation that fails it is an unexpected error of its contribution in the step `operation_check`: the tick is computed again without the contribution, which takes `pause_on_processing_error`, and the commit goes ahead for the others. The check admits the transitions of the table only. A test scans the source: no module other than `recovery.py` writes an operation's status.
